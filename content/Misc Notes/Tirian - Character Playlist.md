## Song Notes

### 1. Please Please Please Let Me Get What I Want — Deftones
*(cover of The Smiths)*

Restrained, desperate longing — the sound of someone who keeps asking and keeps being denied. Tirian has lost everything that mattered: his family, his education at Strixhaven, Gideon. The Deftones cover adds weight and tension that the original doesn't have — there's something darker underneath the asking. Fits the contrast between the polished public Tirian and the seething, controlled Noctis underneath.

**Fit: 8/10**

---

### 2. Mad — YUNGBLUD

The duality song. "Everybody wishing I'd be something that I never have" — Tirian performs the warm, beloved noble prince every day, and does it genuinely, but Noctis is the truth underneath. "I fight myself so I'm less alone" is the double life in five words: two identities at war, neither one fully able to rest. "Detached, unemotional" maps to Noctis specifically — the calculated, cold side of him that the public never sees. Slightly held back from a 9 because Tirian is more composed than the song's chaotic energy suggests — he's not losing control, he's *maintaining* it.

**Fit: 8/10**

---

### 3. Everybody Wants to Rule the World — Lorde
*(cover of Tears for Fears)*

"Nothing ever lasts forever" hits differently for someone whose deepest fear is being forgotten — he watched his entire house disappear in a single night. The melancholy of Lorde's version carries the weight Tirian actually lives under, even when he's charming people at galas. "It's my own desire, it's my own remorse" captures his moral complexity: he chose this path, he carries the cost, and he doesn't ask for sympathy. The line about making the most of freedom and pleasure fits his public persona exactly: the dancing, the charm, the enjoyment of the life he's building — even as the shadow work happens after dark.

**Fit: 9/10**

---

### 4. No Longer You — Jorge Rivera-Herrans & Mason Olshavsky
*(from Epic: The Musical)*

This song is about survival that costs you your identity — the person who lived through it is not the person who went in. Tirian survived the Dawnmere Slaughter. His family didn't. The 20-year-old Silverquill student with two living parents, a grandmother, a little brother and sister — that person doesn't exist anymore. Noctis is what filled the space. "A brother's final stand" — Finn was 16 years old. "Portrayals of betrayal" — someone ordered it. The whole song is a eulogy for who Tirian used to be, delivered to his face. Devastating precision.

**Fit: 9.5/10**

---

### 5. On My Own — Ashes Remain

The emotional truth is right — Tirian has carried this alone for three years, and "come and find me in the dark now" is almost too literal for Noctis. The pride prison is real: his need to handle it himself, to hold the house together solo, to never let the mask slip. "I was never meant to fight on my own" reads like the moment he finds his Campaign 2 companions. Docked slightly because the devotional framing doesn't fully match Tirian's relationship with faith — he's affiliated with the Church of Light for political and genuine reasons, but he's not a true believer.

**Fit: 7.5/10**

---

### 6. The Line — Twenty One Pilots
*(from Arcane / League of Legends)*

Almost written for him. "Please don't let them see me" is Noctis in four words — the entire identity built around not being seen. "I can feel the light shine on my face" and then the shadow underneath: that is Tirian's aurora magic as public mask and Noctis as the truth. "Did I disappoint you?" carries three years of grief toward a family he couldn't save. And "will they still let me over if I cross the line?" is his moral philosophy made into a question — he lies, manipulates, kills when he judges it necessary, and somewhere underneath he's asking whether that still makes him who he thinks he is.

**Fit: 9/10**

---

### 7. Black Sorrow — Park Byeong Hoon
*(Alien Stage OST Part 4)*

The Alien Stage context is inseparable from why this works. That series is about humans forced to perform in deadly competitions for alien entertainment — bonds forged between people who might have to destroy each other, grief that isn't a wound but a condition. The sorrow isn't something that happens to the characters; it's the water they live in. Tirian's grief after the Dawnmere Slaughter works the same way — it's not background noise, it's structural. It shapes every choice he makes. Noctis literally operates in black shadow. The forced performance parallel runs deep: Tirian plays the beloved noble prince every single day for an audience that doesn't know what it costs him.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 8. Never Enough — Loren Allred
*(from The Greatest Showman)*

Two readings at once, and both land.

**The ambition reading:** Tirian's deepest fear is being forgotten. He is actively building a legacy, treating it like a work of art. But there's a hunger underneath that can't be satisfied — no amount of legacy, influence, or political power fills the void. He could become the most remembered name in Brittanian history and it would still feel like not enough, because what he actually wants cannot be returned.

**The grief reading:** No revenge fills it either. He could kill every person who ordered the massacre, restore House Dawnmere to glory, see justice done — and Finn, Lyra, his mother, his father, his grandmother still won't be there. "These hands could hold the world but it'll never be enough" is exactly what that kind of grief does to a person.

And then: "all the shine of a thousand spotlights," "stars we steal from the night sky" — that is aurora magic. Literally. Public Tirian IS the shine. And it's still not enough.

**Fit: 9/10**

---

### 9. Falling Inside the Black — Skillet

"Falling in the black" is Noctis — shadow magic, the darkness, the identity he descended into after the massacre. "Slippin' through the cracks" is the double life itself: existing in the margins, operating where no one can see. But "can I ever go back?" is the line that does the real damage — it connects directly to No Longer You. The question of whether the transformation is permanent, whether the person he was at Strixhaven with a whole family still alive is retrievable or just gone. "Dreaming of the way it used to be" is the grief in its quietest form. "Can you hear me?" is the isolation of three years carrying this alone — grief with no one to land on.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 10. Control — Halsey

One of the strongest fits in the playlist. "God damn right, you should be scared of me" — Noctis is an urban legend. People ARE scared of him. Tirian built that deliberately. "Who is in control?" is the identity fracture question at its sharpest: is Tirian operating Noctis, or has Noctis started to define Tirian? The longer he lives the double life, the harder that question gets to answer. "I turned all the mirrors around" — the inability to face what he's become underneath the noble exterior. And then the bridge: villains begging to be written so they'll never die when he's dead. This lands differently for someone whose deepest fear is being forgotten. The dark parts of him want permanence too — Noctis wants legacy just as much as Tirian does, and they might not always want the same things.

**Fit: 9/10**

---

### 11. Drown — Bring Me the Horizon

The strongest fit in the entire playlist alongside Godlight. "What doesn't kill you makes you wish you were dead" is the darkest inversion of the cliché — and it's more honest than the cliché for a survivor. Tirian didn't come out of the Dawnmere Slaughter stronger in any clean way. He came out as Noctis, carrying something he can never put down. "Got a hole in my soul growing deeper and deeper" is the grief void that revenge doesn't fill — three years in and it's not closing, it's expanding. "The weight of the world's getting harder to hold up" is Tirian holding House Dawnmere, the investigation, the double life, and all the grief, alone. The waves framing is true to how grief actually moves — not constant, but always coming back. And "won't you drag the lake and bring me home again?" is the most vulnerable line in the playlist: underneath the control, underneath Noctis, there is a version of Tirian that wants to be found.

**Fit: 9.5/10**

---

### 12. Doors — Noah Kahan

The intimacy song of the playlist — a different angle from the grief and ambition entries. "It gets harder to see me the closer you try to look" is exactly how the double identity works with anyone who cares about him. Getting closer doesn't reveal Tirian — it reveals another layer of performance. Every door opened leads to another. The public noble, the grief underneath, Noctis behind that, and whoever existed before all of it deepest in. "I just live here, but you're the one who decided to knock" — he didn't ask to be pursued. People choose him knowing something is guarded. "Listing reasons you should pack all your shit up" is the self-sabotage of someone who has lost everyone — already running the exit math for you before you find the door yourself.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 13. King for a Day — Pierce the Veil

Sharp fit across multiple layers. "I don't wanna feel a thing anymore" is the emotional exhaustion of year three — not giving up, but bone-deep tired of carrying something this heavy for this long. "A single night without a ghost in the walls" — Tirian is haunted. Finn at 16, Lyra at 12, his mother, his father, his grandmother. One night without the weight of them pressing through the walls. "We are the shadows screaming, 'Take us now!'" is Noctis in four words. And the closing line is the Tirian philosophy distilled: being forgotten is the only real death. Better to burn out than slowly rust into nothing. He would agree with that completely.

**Fit: 9/10**

---

### 14. Viva la Vida — Coldplay

The fall-from-grace read, applied sideways. Tirian didn't rule the world — but he had one: a family, a future, Strixhaven, standing in the noble circles of Brittania. Then one night it was gone. "Sweep the streets I used to own" is the morning after the massacre in one image. "People couldn't believe what I'd become" — Noctis, the shadow urban legend that emerged from the golden prince. The revolutionaries waiting for his head maps to the people who ordered the assassination — still out there, still waiting. "Who would ever wanna be king?" is the dark underside of his legacy ambition: the loneliness of the position, the puppet strings of noble obligation, the isolation of being the last one standing. He wants the legacy. He might not always want what comes with it.

**Fit: 8/10**

---

### 15. Who We Are — Hozier

Short and precise — the depth is in the compression. "Carving through the dark" is Noctis made metaphor and also literal: shadow magic, operating at night, three years moving through grief and investigation without a map. "To get so far" acknowledges the distance traveled since the massacre — the progress, the surviving, the building. But "the hardest part is who we are" is the thesis of the entire playlist in five words. Not what happened. Not what he does. The question underneath everything: is he Tirian? Is he Noctis? The heir of a dead house? Who he was before any of this? "We're born at night" — Noctis didn't exist before the massacre. That identity was born in darkness.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 16. Godlight — Noah Kahan

The only 10 in the playlist — because it's the only song that holds the entire character at once rather than one piece at a time.

"A hole I can't fill, a curse I can't break" — the grief void, still open three years on. "I gave my soul to it and I cannot be reclaimed" — Noctis. The mission. The shadow work he can't undo. "I was younger then" — the person at Strixhaven with a whole family. Gone. "To know me is to hate me" — if people truly saw underneath the golden prince, the manipulation, the ruthlessness, the shadow vigilante — would they still look at him the way they do? He suspects not. "I'm not the way I was" is No Longer You in four words.

And then the turn. After everything — the hole, the curse, the soul given over, the self-knowledge — there is the Godlight. The look in people's eyes when the legacy lands. When his name means something. When he is seen in that specific light. "The one thing you wanted all your life. It's all mine." The thing Tirian fears losing above everything else. The reason Noctis exists. Not being forgotten.

The song holds the darkness and the hunger in the same breath without resolving either. So does Tirian.

**Fit: 10/10**

---

### 17. Little Lion Man — Mumford & Sons

The accountability song — and it hits differently for someone who is usually in control of everything. Tirian plans, calculates, plays the long game. But there's a version of this song that belongs to Finn: a 16-year-old who idolized his brother, whose heart was very much on the line, and who Tirian couldn't save. "I really fucked it up this time" from someone who almost never admits fault — the rare crack in the composure. Could also map forward to anyone who gets close and gets hurt as a consequence of being close to him. The Mumford rawness, the folk anguish, cuts against the elegant noble exterior in a way that feels like the private Tirian rather than the public one.

**Fit: 8/10**

---

### 18. Jigsaw Falling into Place — Radiohead
*(scene: a ballroom, identity of the other person unclear)*

A scene song rather than a character song — which earns its own category. Tirian is at home in ballrooms and galas; he is charming, magnetic, a natural dance partner. This is the moment of a specific kind of recognition across a crowded room — two people clocking each other, neither quite able to look away, the jigsaw click of something understood without words. "You got the light, you can feel it on your back" works beautifully against the aurora imagery, the way Tirian moves through a formal setting like he was built for it. "Wish away the nightmare" is the undertow — the weight he's carrying, and the brief wish, in this moment, that it wasn't there. The identity of the other person is deliberately open. It'll fit when it fits.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 19. Monster — Imagine Dragons

The transformation fear made explicit. "If I told you what I was" is the Noctis question in its rawest form — not whether people fear him, but whether the people who *love* the golden prince would still look at him the same way if they knew. "Everything I touch isn't dark enough — that this problem lies in me" is the shadow magic reading: something in him is drawn to the darkness, is fluent in it, maybe is it. The man with a candle trying to escape what's inside him — holding the aurora light forward while the shadow follows behind — is the dual magic identity in one image. The monster isn't a villain. It's just what happens when survival requires becoming something you didn't plan to become.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 20. Numb — Marina

The key line: "I got dark only to shine." One sentence that might be the most precise description of Tirian's entire arc. He descended into shadow — became Noctis, built the double life, did the dark work — in service of the light. House Dawnmere's legacy is the shine. The aurora is the shine. Being remembered is the shine. The darkness was never the goal; it was the cost. The sacrifice of who he was frames as "reasonable" — because for Tirian it genuinely is. That's the moral calculus he lives by. The numbness is the anesthetic that makes it livable. You can carry something this heavy if you stop feeling the weight.

**Fit: 9.5/10**

---

### 21. United in Grief — Kendrick Lamar

The counting of days is what earns this entry. One thousand eight hundred and fifty-five days — grief made mathematical, specific, relentless. Tirian has been running this investigation for three years, every day numbered. The uncertainty underneath all that accumulated time — circling the same wound, waking up to another appointment, "what am I doing" beneath the controlled surface — is honest interior for someone who presents as composed. "The world that I'm in is a cul-de-sac" is the investigation going nowhere, the grief that doesn't move, the life reduced to obligations and starting over every morning. The tonal departure from the rest of the playlist is itself the point — grief doesn't always sound like what you expect it to.

**Fit: 8/10**

---

### 22. Karma Police — Radiohead

Three movements, all precise. The first is the exhaustion of having given everything for three years and it still not being enough — the mission continues, the obligation doesn't release, the payroll doesn't end. The second is Noctis as consequence: House Dawnmere was destroyed, and what followed was not grief alone but retribution. The shadow is the answer to what was done. The third — "for a minute there, I lost myself" — is the identity fracture in its barest form. Not a dramatic unraveling. Just a minute. Just a slip. The question of which one is real — Tirian or Noctis — doesn't announce itself. It just happens quietly, then passes, and he keeps going.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 23. The Winner Takes It All — ABBA

A song about the cold mathematics of loss — and the massacre was exactly that. His family fell so someone else could take everything. The political world of Brittania runs on this logic: someone wins, someone pays for it, the calculation is made by people with power and settled on people without it. The bitter resignation underneath — why complain when this is simply how the game is played — is the inversion of Tirian's entire character. He refuses the resignation. He refuses to accept that the outcome is final. But the song knows, even if he doesn't, that the world operates this way regardless.

**Fit: 8/10**

---

### 24. The Cure — Olivia Rodrigo

The relationship song that's really about something deeper than a relationship. The poison in the bloodstream is the grief, the shadow work, the damage accumulated across three years — and someone tries to love it out of him. It helps. It feels like something. But it won't ever be the cure, because what's wrong with Tirian doesn't have a relational answer. The massacre didn't happen because he wasn't loved enough. The void won't close because someone loves him now. Connects directly to Drown — the hole that deepens rather than fills — and to Doors: intimacy can't reach where the damage actually lives. Love is real and good and still not sufficient. Because it was never love that broke it.

**Fit: 8/10**

---

### 25. Seventeen Going Under — Sam Fender

The public armor song. The narrator describes spending his teens in silent internal spiraling while presenting as the grinning joker — humor as shield, the performance so good nobody questioned it. Then the observer delivers the line nobody in the room says: the kid looks so sad. That's Tirian. The warm, charming, beloved noble who is so fluent at the performance that nobody voices what's underneath. His kindness is genuine; the armor is also genuine; neither cancels the other. The joker who is actually drowning is one of the most specific portraits in the playlist of who he is in a room full of people who love him.

**Fit: 8.5/10**

---

### 26. All Them Horses — Noah Kahan

Three images doing heavy lifting at once. The photograph: everyone looks happy, and Tirian's public life IS the photograph — the galas, the aurora, the warmth — real and curated simultaneously, still not the full picture. The county line: he has crossed points of no return. Noctis exists. People are dead by his hand. There is no undoing who he has become. And the last: always on his own. Three years. The double life that prevents real closeness. The plainest statement of the isolation at the centre of everything.

**Fit: 7.5/10**

---

### 27. This Is War — 30 Seconds to Mars

The declaration song — the mission at its most stripped and absolute. Not grief, not identity, not duality: the war itself. Good and evil, soldier and civilian, martyr and victim, everyone implicated in what is coming. The song distinguishes between the moment to lie and the moment to live and the moment to die — and Tirian lives in all three simultaneously. Noctis is the moment to lie. The investigation is the moment to fight. The massacre was the moment that started all of it. The commitment is total: to the edge of the earth if necessary. This is the external-facing song in the playlist — not the interior, but the declaration.

**Fit: 7.5/10**

---

### 28. Non-Stop — Hamilton
*(Original Broadway Cast)*

The running-out-of-time song. Tirian operates with the urgency of someone who understands that time is limited — legacy built now or never, the investigation always pressing, the double life always demanding more. The chess player, the Silverquill rhetorician, the man who walks into every room already knowing how it ends. The question of why he assumes he's the smartest in the room is honest — he usually is, and he knows it. Burr's warning is the interesting part: competence and confidence held together long enough can shade into arrogance, and arrogance makes you blind to what you didn't account for. The doom it predicts isn't certain. But it's not impossible either.

**Fit: 8.5/10**
