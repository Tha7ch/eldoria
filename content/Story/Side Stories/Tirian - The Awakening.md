## The Awakening

It starts the way the good ones always do, with everyone at the table.

Grandmother is at the head, the way she always is, sitting straight in her chair with her hands folded around a cup of tea that's probably gone cold. She never drinks it. She just holds it. The same way she holds a room: effortlessly, like she doesn't notice she's doing it. Her hair is silver-white tonight, falling over one shoulder, and the light from the candles catches it and makes it glow.

Father is beside her. He's leaning back in his chair with his arms crossed, which means he's pretending to be stern about something. His jaw is set and his brows are low, but his eyes are warm. They always are when he thinks no one is looking. He's big. He fills his chair the way he fills every room he walks into, and his voice is deep and steady and it makes the walls feel solid.

Mother is laughing. I can hear it before I see her. Her laugh is the kind that starts quiet and then catches, like she didn't mean to let it out but couldn't help it. She's sitting across from Father, leaning forward with one hand on the table, and her eyes are bright. She's beautiful. She's always beautiful, but right now, with the candlelight on her face and that laugh still fading, she looks like something from a painting.

Finn is next to me. He's four, and he's too small for the chair, so he's kneeling on it with his elbows on the table and his chin in his hands. He's watching Grandmother with his mouth slightly open, the way he does when he's trying very hard to follow something that's a little above him. His hair is a mess. It's always a mess. Mother fixes it three times a day and it's back to chaos within the hour.

I'm here. I'm eight. I'm sitting between Finn and Father, and the table is warm and the food smells good and everyone is talking and I'm home.

This is the part I want to stay in.

---

Grandmother is telling a story. I think it's about the old house, the one before this one, the one her grandmother built. She's talking about the gardens, about how the ice roses used to bloom in winter and catch the northern lights in their petals. Her hands move when she talks, slow and deliberate, like she's shaping the memory in the air in front of her.

"They grew in rows along the eastern wall," she says. "Your great-great-grandmother planted the first ones herself. She said they needed to face the dawn."

"Everything in this family needs to face the dawn," Father says. Half joke, half something he actually believes. Grandmother gives him a look that could mean anything, and Mother laughs again.

Finn tugs on my sleeve. "Tirian. Do you think Grandmother will make the lights tonight?"

"Maybe. If you ask her nicely."

"I always ask nicely." He doesn't. He bounces on his knees and grabs her arm and says *please please please* until she gives in. It works every time. Not because he's polite, but because Grandmother loves him and he knows it.

He goes back to pushing a piece of bread around his plate with his finger, and I watch him and I think: I want to learn how to do that. The lights. The aurora. The colors that Grandmother pulls from the air like they were always there, just waiting for her hands. I've watched her do it a hundred times. I want to hold them myself. The blues and greens and faint pinks, the colors of the sky breaking apart and reforming, alive and shifting and beautiful. I want to be able to do that for Finn. For all of them.

The candles are warm. The food is good. Father is telling Mother something about the south wing and she's pretending to care about masonry. Grandmother is watching all of us with that look she gets sometimes, quiet and satisfied, like she's seeing something that isn't quite in the room but makes her happy anyway.

I want to remember this.

---

The candlelight doesn't reach me.

I notice it the way you notice a draft. Not all at once, but as a feeling that something shifted. The table is bright. Grandmother's face is lit. Father's hands are golden where they rest on the wood. But my side of the table is dimmer. Just slightly. Just enough that if I weren't paying attention, I wouldn't see it.

I am paying attention.

I look at the candles. They're burning fine, tall and steady, the same as always. The light is leaving them in every direction. It just... stops, a little sooner on my side. Like something is catching it before it gets to me.

It's nothing. It's how candles work. Light is uneven. I know this.

I pick up my fork.

The food has no taste.

It looks the same. Roasted potatoes, the kind Mother makes with rosemary and salt, the ones I've eaten a hundred times. They're warm on the plate. They steam when I break one open. But when I put it in my mouth there's nothing. No salt, no rosemary, no warmth. It's like chewing something that was never food at all. Just the shape of food, the memory of what it's supposed to be, with everything real pulled out of it.

I swallow. I take another bite. Nothing.

I look up. No one else has noticed. Father is still talking. Mother is still smiling. Finn is still kneeling on his chair with bread crumbs on his chin. Everything is the same. Everything is fine.

I put my fork down.

Under the table, the shadows are too deep.

I don't know why I look. I don't know what makes me glance down at my feet, at the floor beneath the table. But I do, and the dark down there is wrong. Too thick. Too solid. The kind of dark that shouldn't exist in a room with this many candles burning. It looks like someone poured ink across the floorboards and it settled into the grain, pooling between the chair legs, gathering in the corners where the table meets the floor.

It's darker where I'm sitting.

My feet are in it. My shoes are lost in it. I can't see where the floor ends and the shadow begins, and when I pull my foot back the dark doesn't move. It stays where it is, dense and still, like water too heavy to ripple.

Something is wrong.

I look up at the table. At my family. At the people I love more than anything in the world.

Grandmother's hands are glowing.

It starts faint. A soft light at her fingertips, like the last ember of a candle caught beneath her skin. Then it spreads. It moves up her wrists, along her arms, and the glow deepens into color. Blues and greens and silvers, the aurora light that is hers, that has always been hers. It spills from her like breath, like it costs her nothing, and her face is calm and beautiful and she doesn't even seem to notice.

Father is next. The glow starts at his chest, warm and gold, steady, the color of dawn breaking. It fills him the way his voice fills a room, solid and certain, and his skin is radiant with it. He looks like what he is. The head of the house. The man the name belongs to, lit from the inside by something that was born in him and will pass through him to us.

Mother. Her light is softer. Pale and gentle, like moonlight on snow. It starts at her shoulders and cascades down her arms, and when she reaches across the table to touch Father's hand, the two lights meet and blend. Gold and silver weaving together.

Finn. Even Finn. He's four years old and too young for any of this, but the dream doesn't care about that. A glow starts in his small hands, faint and unsteady, flickering, but it's there. Aurora light. The family light. He's laughing and he doesn't know why, and the glow pulses with his laughter.

Everyone is shining.

I look down at my hands.

---

They're dark.

Not unlit. Not just the absence of the glow everyone else is carrying. *Dark.* Actively dark, like the air around my fingers has thickened and turned and is pulling the light out of the room. Wisps of black curl from my knuckles, slow and silent, drifting upward like smoke from a fire that isn't burning.

I stare at them. I can't move.

The dark is spreading. Creeping up my wrists, along the inside of my arms, and it's cold. Not the cold I know. Not the clean sharp cold of ice and snow and the magic Grandmother makes. This cold is heavy. It sits on my skin like something wet. It has weight and it has intent and it is coming from inside me.

I try to speak. I try to say *Mother*.

Nothing comes out. My mouth opens and the word is there, right there, I can feel the shape of it on my tongue, but the sound won't come. It's swallowed before it leaves. Pulled inward, taken by the same dark that's crawling up my arms.

The room is dimming. My family is still glowing, brighter now, all of them, the aurora light intensifying, but the edges of the room are going dark. The walls are pulling away. The candlelight is shrinking. The table is getting smaller. The distance between me and them is growing, even though no one has moved.

Finn is right next to me and he's a thousand miles away.

I try again. *Mother.* I push it out with everything I have, every part of my chest, but there's nothing. Just the silence and the dark and the growing cold and my family shining in a light I can't reach, can't touch, can't—

---

I wake up.

My hands. I'm staring at my hands.

The dark is still there.

For a moment I don't understand. I'm not at the table anymore. I can feel the bed beneath me, the sheets tangled around my legs, the pillow damp with sweat against my neck. I'm in my room. I'm awake. I'm awake and the dark is still coming out of my hands.

Wisps of shadow curl from my fingers. Slow. Quiet. Real. They drift upward from my skin like they belong there, like they've always been there and I just couldn't see them before. The cold is the same cold from the dream. Heavy. Dense. Wrong. Not aurora-cold, not the crisp clean bite of ice that I've felt a hundred times watching Grandmother work. This is something else. Something that sits in the air and makes the air heavier.

My heart is hammering so hard I can feel it in my throat.

I look up.

The shadows of the room have moved.

The dark under my dresser, the thin strip of black that's always there, that I've never once thought about, that is just what happens when furniture meets floor... it's wider. Thicker. It's reached out across the floorboards toward the foot of my bed like something poured from a vessel. The shadow behind my curtain has pooled outward. The corner by the door, the one that's always a little dim even with the window open, has risen. It's climbed halfway up the wall, a darkness so deep it looks like the stone itself has been replaced with nothing.

And all of it is moving toward me.

Not fast. Not like something attacking. Slow and steady, drawn inward, the way water moves toward a drain. Like I'm the lowest point in the room and everything dark is flowing downhill to meet me.

I am the source.

The realization hits like a hand around my throat. This isn't happening to me. I'm not caught in something. I am *doing* this. The shadows on the floor are mine. The dark on my hands is mine. The cold, the weight, the pulling. All of it is coming from me, out of me, and I don't know how and I don't know why and I can't—

I can't—

I clench my fists.

Both hands, as tight as I can. My nails dig into my palms and the pain is sharp and real and mine and I hold on to it. I squeeze my eyes shut. I hold my breath. I push it down.

It doesn't want to go.

It's like swallowing something too large for my throat. Something alive, something that pushes back. I can feel it in my chest, in my arms, in the roots of my fingers where the shadow wisps are still curling. It resists. It presses against the inside of my ribs like a second heartbeat, heavy and slow and wrong, and I push harder. I grit my teeth until my jaw aches. I press my fists against my chest and I force it down, down, back into wherever it came from.

It goes.

Not all at once. Slowly. Reluctantly. Like something retreating behind a door that I'm holding shut with both hands. The cold recedes from my skin. The wisps stop. The air thins. The shadows on the floor slow their crawl and settle. Still too deep, still too thick, but no longer moving.

I open my eyes.

My hands are shaking. My whole body is shaking. The room is dark and still and the shadows are just shadows again. Almost. Almost normal. Almost the way they're supposed to be.

But I can feel it. Behind whatever I just closed, behind whatever wall I built with nothing but panic and clenched fists, it's still there. Waiting. Not gone. Not even sleeping. Just... held. Like a door I'm leaning against with everything I have, and I can feel the pressure on the other side, patient and constant. And I know, with a certainty I have never felt about anything in my life, that if I stop pushing, it will open.

I don't move.

I don't breathe.

I sit in the dark with my fists pressed against my chest and I wait, because I don't know what else to do. I'm eight years old and something came out of me that I don't have a name for. Something that is the opposite of everything my family is. And I am alone in my room and I cannot tell anyone.

I can never tell anyone.

---

I don't know how long I sit like that. Long enough for my hands to stop shaking. Long enough for my heartbeat to slow from a sprint to something that only feels like running. Long enough for the sweat on my neck to go cold and dry and for the sheets to stop feeling damp.

When I finally move, it's just my eyes.

The window.

The curtain is half open. It's always half open; I like the moonlight. Beyond it the sky is changing. The black is thinning at the edge, going grey, then pale, and then, slowly, so slowly, warm.

The sun is rising.

It comes up over the eastern wall of the estate. The same wall Grandmother talked about in the dream, the one where the ice roses used to grow. The light spills over the stone and into the courtyard and it's gold. Real gold. Not the cold sharp kind but the soft kind, the kind that warms your skin and makes the dust in the air look like it's floating instead of falling.

I watch it. I can't look away.

The light reaches my window. It comes through the glass and touches the floor of my room, and the shadows pull back from it the normal way. The way shadows are supposed to move: retreating because the light is brighter, not because something told them to. It reaches my bed. It reaches my hands.

I open my fists. My palms are marked with red crescents where my nails dug in. The skin is pale and clean and there is no dark on it. No wisps. No cold. Just my hands, small and ordinary, in a bar of morning sunlight.

I hold them there.

I hold them in the light and I don't move and I don't think about what happened and I don't think about what it means. I just feel the warmth on my skin, real warmth, the kind that reaches all the way through, and I stay there, because right now it's the only thing in the world that feels like the opposite of what I am.

The sun keeps rising. I hear the house stirring below me. Footsteps. The faint sound of someone opening a door. A voice I think is Mother's, calling something I can't quite make out.

I don't move.

I stay in the light.

It's all I have.
