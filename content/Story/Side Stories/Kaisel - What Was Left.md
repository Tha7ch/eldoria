# ***Part 3***

 Two days after meeting Veyr, I arrived in the town where her sister was supposed to live.

 It was a lot calmer than Black Lantern Row, which was a very low bar to clear. The streets were wide enough for carriages to pass through without someone getting shoved into a wall, the buildings were painted in warm colors, and the smell of fresh bread drifted out from one of the shops near the main road. People actually smiled here. Children ran past with wooden swords in their hands, merchants called out prices from behind their stalls, and an old man swept dust from the front of his store like that was the most important thing happening in the world.

 It almost made me feel overdressed.

 I walked through the town with Veyr's note in one hand and my other hand resting near the strap across my chest. My sword sat across my back, the hilt high near my shoulder, and every now and then someone looked at it before looking at me. Unlike Black Lantern Row, most people didn't whisper. They just stared for a second, realized who I was, and then tried to act like they hadn't.

 I couldn't blame them. I did look pretty important.

 The sky above the town was still mostly clear, but I could smell the rain before I saw it. I stopped near the center of the road and lifted my head slightly, breathing in through my nose. The air had that wet, cold smell that always came before a storm, like the clouds were already leaking somewhere far away.

 I looked toward the distance.

 Dark clouds were rolling in from beyond the hills, thick and heavy, slowly swallowing the pale blue sky. They were still far enough away that most people hadn't noticed yet, but they were coming. The wind had shifted too, carrying a chill that made the banners above the street snap lightly against their poles.

 "Great," I muttered.

 I hated rain.

 Not in a dramatic way or anything. I just hated being wet. Wet clothes stuck to everything, boots got heavy, hair got annoying, and somehow everyone always expected you to keep doing your job like water wasn't falling from the sky for no reason.

 I looked down at the note again.

 Mira's house wasn't far.

 "Alright," I said under my breath, folding the paper and slipping it into my pocket. "Let's get this done before the sky decides to ruin my day."

 The house was on a quieter road near the edge of town. It didn't stand out much, which was probably the point. Two stories, pale stone walls, dark wooden beams, a small garden in front, and a narrow path leading to the door. There were a few flowerpots sitting beneath the windows, though most of the flowers inside them had started to wilt.

 I stopped at the front gate and looked around.

 Nothing looked wrong.

 No broken windows. No signs of a struggle. No blood on the stones. No shady figures hiding behind the fence. Just a normal house in a normal town, sitting quietly beneath a sky that was about to open up.

 Honestly, that made me more suspicious.

 I walked up the path and knocked on the door.

 No answer.

 I waited a few seconds, then knocked again, harder this time.

 Still nothing.

 "Mira?" I called. "You home?"

 The street behind me stayed quiet. A woman carrying a basket passed by on the other side of the road and gave me a curious look. I gave her a quick smile, mostly to let her know I wasn't robbing the place, then turned back to the door.

 I tried the handle.

 It opened.

 That was never a good sign.

 I stepped inside slowly.

 "Mira?" I called again, pushing the door open with the back of my hand. "Your sister sent me. I'm not here to cause trouble."

 The first thing I noticed was the smell.

 It wasn't rot. It wasn't blood. It wasn't anything I could point to and say, yes, that's the problem. The house smelled like dust, old wood, and something faintly sour underneath it all. Like a room that had been closed for too long.

 I took a few steps inside and shut the door behind me.

 The entryway opened into a small sitting room. A couch sat near the window, covered in a faded blue blanket. A low table stood in the center of the room with an empty cup on it. Shelves lined one wall, filled with books, little carved figures, and a few framed sketches. Nothing looked disturbed.

 Still, something felt wrong.

 I stood there for a moment, trying to figure out what it was.

 The house wasn't silent exactly. Wood creaked. Wind brushed against the windows. Somewhere upstairs, something shifted slightly, maybe the building settling, maybe not. But beneath all of that, there was this strange feeling sitting in my chest. Not fear. Not exactly. More like my body had noticed something before my head did.

 I didn't like that.

 "Mira?" I called again, moving deeper into the house. "If you're here, now would be a great time to say something. Your sister's worried, and I'd rather not have to explain that I broke into your house because your door was unlocked."

 Nothing.

 I checked the sitting room first. The blanket on the couch was folded neatly, the cup on the table was dry, and a book sat open near the window. I leaned over and glanced at it, but it was just a collection of old poems. Not exactly useful unless the mystery involved bad rhymes.

 The kitchen was normal too. A few dishes stacked near the wash basin. Some herbs hanging from the ceiling. A small loaf of bread wrapped in cloth on the counter. It was hard, but not rotten. That told me someone had been here recently, or at least recently enough.

 I moved through the dining area next, then checked a small storage room behind it. Nothing. No signs of a fight. No hidden bodies. No creepy messages carved into the walls.

 Good.

 Probably.

 I went upstairs.

 The stairs creaked under my boots, each step sounding louder than it should have. At the top, a narrow hallway stretched toward three rooms. One was a bedroom. One was a study. One was a small washroom.

 I checked the bedroom first.

 The bed was made, but not perfectly. A shawl hung over the back of a chair. A comb sat on a dresser beside a small box of jewelry. The room looked lived in, but not abandoned. That made the empty house feel even stranger.

 In the study, papers covered the desk in uneven stacks. Some were letters. Some were notes. Most looked like everyday things. Bills, lists, reminders. I skimmed through them quickly, looking for anything weird, but nothing jumped out at me.

 The washroom was empty.

 By the time I came back downstairs, the sky outside had grown darker. Gray light filtered through the windows, and the room looked colder than it had when I first entered. The wind picked up outside, rattling one of the shutters.

 "Wonderful," I muttered. "Because this wasn't creepy enough."

 I returned to the dining area and paused.

 There was something on the table.

 I hadn't noticed it before, which immediately annoyed me. It was a dagger, or at least shaped like one. The blade was thin and curved in a strange way, almost uneven, with markings carved along the metal that didn't look like any language I knew. The handle was black, wrapped in old leather, and the guard curved outward like little hooks.

 I stepped closer.

 The feeling in my chest got worse.

 I stared at the dagger for a few seconds before reaching toward it.

 It kind of looked like my swor—

 A quiet cough came from behind me.

 I froze.

 Slowly, I turned.

 A woman stood in the doorway to the sitting room.

 She looked like Veyr.

 Not exactly, but close enough that I knew who she was immediately. Same sharp green eyes. Same freckles across her cheeks. Same dark red hair, though Mira's was longer and fell past her shoulders in loose waves. She was wearing a simple dark dress, and one hand rested lightly against the doorframe.

 She didn't look scared.

 That was the first thing I noticed.

 If a stranger with a sword was standing in my house, I felt like I'd be at least a little concerned.

 "Who are you?" she asked. Her voice was calm.

 Too calm.

 I lowered my hand away from the dagger and turned fully toward her.

 "Kaisel Irvenest," I said. "Your sister sent me."

 Mira's expression barely changed.

 "Veyr sent you?"

 "Yeah. She said she hadn't heard from you in a while. Wanted me to make sure you were okay."

 For a moment, Mira simply looked at me.

 Then her gaze moved past me toward the dagger on the table.

 "I'm fine."

 "You sure?"

 "As you can see."

 She stepped into the room slowly. Her movements were smooth, quiet, almost careful. She looked around her own house like she was checking whether I had touched anything.

 "You can tell my sister I appreciate her concern," she continued, though she didn't sound very appreciative. "But she didn't need to send anyone, especially not someone like you. I'm perfectly capable of taking care of myself."

 I smiled a little.

 "Yeah, I'm getting that impression."

 "Then you can leave."

 That was direct. I looked around the dining room again, then back at her.

 "I probably should ask a few questions first."

 "No, you shouldn't." Her eyes narrowed slightly. "Lord Irvenest, I don't know what my sister told you, but there's nothing here. I'm not missing. I'm not in danger. I'm not being held against my will. You came all this way for nothing."

 I leaned against the edge of the table.

 "Maybe. But your door was unlocked, your house feels weird, and there's a creepy dagger sitting on your dining table. So I'm gonna need at least one explanation before I walk back into the rain."

 Her eyes sharpened at the word dagger. "Don't touch it." 

 I glanced at the blade. "Now I want to touch it."

 "I mean it." For the first time, there was something in her voice. Like she was warning me.

 I straightened slightly. "Mira, what is that?"

 She didn't answer.

 Instead, she looked at me. Really looked at me.

 Her gaze moved from my face to my amulet, then toward the sword on my back. Her expression changed slowly, like she had noticed something she hadn't seen before.

 Then she whispered, "You're like me."

 The words made the room feel colder.

 I frowned. "What?"

 Her head tilted slightly. "You're like me."

 "I don't know what that means."

 For a second, Mira smiled.

 It wasn't her smile. I knew that immediately, even though I had never met her before.

 Then something slammed into my face.

 I didn't even see her move.

 One moment she was standing near the doorway, and the next her hand cracked across my cheek with enough force to send me flying across the room. My body crashed through a chair, slammed into the wall, and dropped hard onto the floor.

 Pain flashed through my skull.

 "Ow," I groaned, pushing myself onto one elbow. "What the hell was that?"

 Mira stood in the same spot.

 No, not the same.

 Her posture had changed.

 Her shoulders were slightly hunched now, her fingers twitching at her sides. Her green eyes darkened, the color shifting as something black bled into the edges. The air around her seemed heavier, like the room had suddenly filled with pressure.

 I grabbed the table and pulled myself to my feet.

 "Mira?"

 Her eyes lifted to mine.

 They weren't green anymore.

 Not fully.

 Black veins spread across the whites of her eyes, and her pupils stretched into thin, ugly slits. Her mouth twitched into a smile that looked wrong on her face.

 "You smell like us," she said.

 I reached over my shoulder and grabbed my sword.

 "I really don't like that sentence."

 "You don't know?"

 "Know what?"

 She laughed softly.

 The sound crawled across my skin.

 "You don't even know what you are."

 I drew my sword and held it low at my side.

 "I'm the Chosen of Wylder." The moment I said it, the smile vanished.

 Mira's face twisted. "Wylder."

 The word came out like a curse.

 Then she rushed me.

 I barely got my blade up in time. Her hand struck the flat of my sword with a sharp crack, and the force pushed me back several steps. I twisted, shoved her arm aside, and stepped away instead of swinging.

 "Mira, stop!"

 She didn't.

 She came at me again, faster this time. Her nails dragged across the table as she vaulted over it, splintering the wood beneath her hand. I ducked under her first swipe, caught her wrist, and tried to turn her away without hurting her.

 She was stronger than she looked.

 Much stronger.

 She twisted out of my grip and kicked me in the stomach. The blow drove the air from my lungs and sent me sliding back into the sitting room. I caught myself against the wall and raised my sword again, keeping the blade between us.

 "Okay," I said, breathing hard. "That's enough."

 Mira stalked toward me slowly.

 Her fingers bent in a weird way. "You fight like you want to save her."

 "That's because I do."

 "She's gone."

 I didn't answer.

 "Gone," Mira repeated, smiling wider. "Gone, gone, gone."

 Thunder rumbled outside.

 I glanced toward the window.

 Rain struck the glass a second later.

 Light at first.

 Then heavier.

 "Of course," I muttered.

 The storm had arrived.

 The room darkened quickly as clouds swallowed the last of the daylight. Rain hammered against the roof, and the windows rattled under the wind. A flash of lightning lit up the room for one bright second, casting Mira's twisted shadow across the wall behind her.

 Then darkness returned.

 She attacked again.

 This time, I could see her.

 That should've been impossible.

 The room was nearly black, broken only by faint gray light from the storm outside, but my eyes adjusted like they were made for it. I could see her movement clearly. The shift of her shoulders. The angle of her wrist. The way her feet touched the ground before she lunged.

 I blocked the first strike, ducked the second, and stepped around the third.

 My body moved before I fully thought about it.

 I frowned as I shoved her back with the guard of my sword.

 "Since when can I see this well in the dark?"

 Mira laughed and lunged again.

 I didn't have time to think about it.

 She moved fast, scraping across the floor with sharp, jerky movements that didn't belong to a person. I kept blocking with the flat of my blade, using my forearm and shoulder to redirect her whenever she got too close. Every time I had an opening, I pulled back.

 I didn't want to hurt her.

 Not yet.

 Not while there was still a chance.

 "Mira!" I shouted over the rain. "If you can hear me, snap out of it!"

 Her answer was a clawed hand raking across my chest. Pain burned hot through me.

 I stumbled back, one hand flying to my chest as blood soaked through the cut in my shirt. It wasn't deep enough to kill me, but it hurt enough to make my vision blur for a second.

 I stared at the blood on my fingers.

 Then at her.

 "Okay," I said, voice lower now. "I'm getting tired of you."

 Mira's body jerked.

 Her shoulders cracked upward.

 Her neck twisted slightly, and the sound it made was awful. Her face started changing, skin tightening across her bones while black lines crawled along her cheeks. Her mouth stretched wider than it should have, and her arms lengthened, fingers dragging near the floor.

 I took one slow step back. "Mira…"

 The thing wearing her body smiled. "Mira isn't here."

 My grip tightened around my sword. "What did you do to her?"

 The creature tilted its head. "She cried for days."

 My stomach dropped. "What did you do?"

 "She called for her sister. Called for help. Wrote little words in her little book, as if ink could save her."

 The rain roared louder against the windows.

 "Answer me."

 "I killed her."

 For a second, I didn't move.

 I didn't breathe.

 The words landed in my chest and stayed there.

 "No."

 "Yes."

 "No."

 "She was weak. Afraid. Alone. And now she is useful."

 Something inside me went very still.

 Up until then, I had been holding back.

 Blocking.

 Dodging.

 Trying to save Mira.

 But Mira was already gone.

 The thing in front of me had just been wearing her face.

 I lowered my sword slightly.

 The demon laughed.

 "There it is. That little anger. That little fire. Do you feel it? Do you feel what's inside you finally waking—"

 "Shut up."

 The room went silent except for the storm.

 I raised my sword again.

 "I'm done playing."

 The demon rushed me.

 This time, I met it.

 Our clash split one of the chairs in half. My blade came down in a bright arc, holy light flashing across the edge as it struck the demon's arm. The creature shrieked and reeled back, smoke rising from the wound.

 I didn't stop.

 I stepped forward and swung again, driving it toward the dining room. It scraped along the floor, climbed half onto the wall like some kind of insect, then kicked off and came at me from above.

 I threw my sword upward.

 Not at it.

 Above it.

 The blade spun once in the air as I grabbed the edge of my coat, yanked it off my shoulders, and tossed it aside. My shirt was already torn and stained with blood, and the rain-cooled air coming through the cracked window hit the cut across my chest.

 I caught my sword as it dropped back down.

 Then I swung.

 The demon blocked with both arms, and the force blasted it through the dining table.

 Wood exploded across the room.

 I breathed hard, eyes locked on it.

 The demon rose from the broken table, laughing through a mouth full of too many teeth.

 "You still don't understand," it said. "You think this body mattered? You think killing this flesh means anything? Mira was weak long before I found her."

 I charged.

 The fight became brutal after that.

 It cut me across the shoulder. I slammed the pommel of my sword into its jaw. It threw me through the kitchen doorway. I drove holy light through my blade and carved a burning line across its side. It screamed, then grabbed me by the throat and hurled me into the stair railing hard enough to crack the wood.

 I hit the floor and rolled onto my side.

 My ears rang.

 My ribs hurt.

 My chest burned.

 I pushed myself up anyway.

 The demon walked toward me slowly, rain and lightning flashing through the windows behind it. Mira's face was barely recognizable now. The thing beneath her skin had stretched her features into something cruel and wrong.

 "You have no clue how powerful I am," it said. "This host was weak, but she lasted long enough. Long enough for me to grow. Long enough for me to wait."

 I spat blood onto the floor. "You talk too much."

 It moved.

 I barely blocked.

 The force knocked me to one knee. My arms shook as I held the sword between us, the demon pressing down with both clawed hands against the blade.

 "Still brave?" it hissed.

 "Still ugly?" I shot back through clenched teeth.

 Its knee drove into my stomach.

 I folded forward, and it slammed its elbow across my back, knocking me fully to the floor. My sword skidded out of my hand and struck the wall several feet away.

 The demon grabbed me by the collar of my torn shirt and dragged me up.

 My feet barely touched the floor.

 It smiled inches from my face.

 "Chosen of Wylder." It said the title like it was mocking me. "Look at you."

 Then it threw me.

 I crashed into the sitting room table and hit the floor hard enough that the room spun. For a few seconds, I couldn't move. I could hear rain. Thunder. Wood creaking. My own breathing.

 The demon approached slowly.

 I tried to push myself up.

 My arm gave out.

 "Damn it," I muttered.

 My sword was too far.

 The demon stood over me. "You should have left when she told you to."

 I forced myself onto one knee. Blood dripped from my mouth onto the floor. 

 My hand shook.

 Not from fear.

 From exhaustion.

 The demon raised one clawed hand.

 I stared up at it, trying to move, trying to reach for something, anything.

 Then something moved inside me.

 It started deep in my chest, cold at first, then hot. My fingers curled against the floor as a strange pressure surged through my arm. It felt like my body had decided to do something before asking me if that was fine.

 "What the…"

 Dark energy crawled over my hand.

 I stared at it.

 Black and red light twisted around my fingers, jagged and violent, snapping like sparks from a broken forge. Other colors flickered for a second, faint blues and greens swallowed almost immediately by the deeper red and black. The air around my palm bent slightly, like the darkness itself was being pulled into shape.

 The demon stopped.

 For the first time, it looked afraid.

 "No."

 I pushed myself to my feet.

 My body moved almost on its own.

 My arm lifted.

 My palm aimed straight at the demon.

 I had no idea what I was doing.

 No idea what this was.

 But the words came out anyway.

 "Eldritch Blast."

 The energy erupted from my hand.

 A jagged beam of black and red tore across the room, screaming through the air like something alive. It struck the demon in the chest and drove it backward through the shattered furniture. The creature shrieked, its body breaking apart under the blast as the light swallowed it.

 Then it was gone.

 The sound vanished all at once.

 I stood there with my arm still raised, breathing hard.

 Rain hit the windows.

 Smoke drifted from the blackened floor.

 My hand shook.

 I looked at my palm.

 Then at the destroyed room.

 Then back at my palm.

 "What…"

 I swallowed.

 "What the hell was that?"

 Nothing answered.

 I pointed at the wall again.

 Nothing happened.

 I frowned and pointed harder.

 Still nothing.

 "No, seriously," I said, voice hoarse. "Do it again."

 Nothing.

 I stared at my hand for a few more seconds.

 Then a grin started to form despite everything.

 "That was…"

 I looked at the cratered floor where the demon had been.

 "THAT WAS AWESOME!"

 The excitement faded quickly. Because then I remembered Mira.

 The house was quiet after that.

 Too quiet.

 The storm continued outside while I searched through what remained of the rooms. I found no sign of Mira alive. No sign that she had escaped. No sign that anything could be saved.

 Eventually, I found the journal.

 It was in the study upstairs, tucked beneath a loose board under the desk. The leather cover was worn, and the pages inside were filled with neat handwriting that started steady and slowly became shakier toward the end.

 I sat on the floor beside the desk and read.

 At first, the entries were normal. Small things. Town gossip. Complaints about Veyr being overprotective. Notes about bread prices, weather, books she wanted to buy.

 Then they changed.

 She wrote about hearing whispers when she was alone.

 Feeling watched.

 Waking up in rooms she didn't remember entering.

 Losing time.

 Finding scratches on her hands.

 The last few pages were harder to read.

 Not because the words were messy, though they were.

 Because I knew what they meant.

 She knew something was wrong with her.

 She knew she was slipping away.

 One line made me stop completely.

 "I don't think I'll see Veyr again."

 I stared at it for a long time.

 The words blurred slightly.

 I blinked hard, but that didn't help much.

 The last entry was short.

 "If anyone finds this, tell my sister I'm sorry. I didn't want to be alone."

 I closed the journal slowly.

 For a while, I just sat there.

 The storm outside softened into steady rain.

 My chest hurt, and not just from the cut.

 I had come here to check on her.

 That was all.

 Just check on her.

 Instead, I found out she had been alone, scared, and already gone before I even knocked on the door.

 I couldn't save her.

 That thought sat with me longer than I wanted it to.

 Later, after the rain stopped, I buried what was left of Mira behind the house, beneath the small tree in her garden. I didn't know if that was where she would have wanted to rest. I didn't know anything about her, really.

 But it felt better than leaving her in that house.

 The ground was wet and heavy, and every movement made the cuts across my body ache. By the time I finished, my hands were covered in dirt, my shirt was torn beyond saving, and my body felt like one big bruise.

 I placed the journal near the marker I made from two pieces of broken wood.

 A tear slipped down my face before I could stop it.

 I wiped it away quickly.

 Then stood there anyway.

 "I'm sorry," I said quietly.

 There wasn't anything else to say.

 A few days passed before I returned to Veyr.

 Maybe a little more.

 Time got weird after that.

 I remember walking back to the bathhouse with Mira's journal under my arm and every injury on my body complaining at once. I remember Veyr waiting in that same lower room, though she wasn't smiling when I entered.

 I told her everything.

 The house.

 The dagger.

 Mira.

 The demon.

 The journal.

 I left out the strange blast from my hand.

 Not because I was trying to hide it.

 Mostly because I still didn't know how to explain it without sounding insane.

 Veyr didn't interrupt me once.

 When I finished, she took the journal from me and held it against her chest. For a long time, she didn't say anything. Her face stayed still, but her hands were shaking.

 Then she thanked me.

 Not loudly.

 Not dramatically.

 Just quietly.

 She said Mira could rest now.

 I didn't know what to say to that, so I didn't say much.

 After that, I spent almost two weeks recovering.

 I got my ribs checked. I replaced my ruined shirt and coat. I slept more than I wanted to and less than I probably should have. I thought about the demon more than I admitted. I thought about my hand. About the darkness that had crawled over my fingers. About the words I had said without knowing why.

 Eldritch Blast.

 I tried to do it again three times.

 Nothing happened.

 On the fourth try, I nearly knocked over a lamp because I got frustrated and swung my arm too hard.

 So I stopped.

 For now.

 Eventually, when the cuts were closed enough and the bruises faded from purple to yellow, I went home.

 House Irvenest looked the same as it always did.

 Tall stone walls. Wide front steps. Clean windows. Banners hanging from the upper balconies. Guards at the gate who straightened immediately when they saw me approaching.

 I gave them a tired wave.

 One of them opened the gate.

 "Lord Kaisel."

 "Hey."

 He looked at the bandage on my cheek, then the one wrapped around my forearm.

 His mouth opened like he wanted to ask.

 I pointed at him.

 "Don't."

 He closed his mouth.

 Smart guy.

 I walked up the front steps and pushed open the doors.

 "Hey, everyone," I called, stepping into the entrance hall. "I'm home."

 I barely got the words out before someone ran at me.

 "Kaisel!"

 Elizabeth slammed into me with enough force to make my ribs complain immediately.

 I hissed through my teeth but wrapped an arm around her anyway.

 "Ow. Hi."

 She didn't let go.

 Her arms tightened around me, and for a second I realized she was shaking.

 "You're okay," she said, her voice pressed against my shoulder.

 I blinked.

 "Yeah. Why wouldn't I be?"

 She pulled back just enough to look at me. Her eyes were wet, and that made me feel awkward immediately because I had no idea what I had done wrong.

 "You were gone for almost a month."

 "That's not that long."

 She stared at the bandage on my face.

 Then at my arm.

 Then at the healing cut visible beneath my collar.

 "You came back covered in bandages."

 "Okay, that part looks bad."

 "Kaisel."

 "Still alive though."

 She looked like she wanted to hit me and hug me again at the same time.

 Before she could decide, more footsteps filled the hall.

 Roland appeared first, standing near the bottom of the staircase with Catherine beside him. Cedric came from one of the side halls, his usual bored expression gone. Amelia stood behind him, arms crossed, but even she looked worried.

 That was weird.

 Cedric and Amelia almost never looked worried about me.

 Annoyed, sure.

 Disappointed, often.

 Worried?

 Not usually.

 "What's going on?" I asked, still holding Elizabeth with one arm. "Why is everyone looking at me like I died?"

 Nobody answered right away.

 My smile faded.

 Roland stepped forward.

 His face was serious.

 Too serious.

 "Kaisel," he said, voice low. "Where have you been?"

 "On a mission."

 "Without telling us?"

 "It was supposed to be quick."

 Catherine's lips pressed into a thin line.

 "You disappeared for nearly a month and returned injured."

 "I've returned injured before."

 "That is not comforting," Elizabeth said immediately.

 I looked between all of them.

 The air in the room felt strange.

 Heavy.

 Similar to the feeling in Mira's house, but different. This wasn't danger. This was something else.

 "Is everything okay?" I asked.

 Roland and Catherine exchanged a look.

 That made my stomach tighten.

 Catherine stepped closer.

 "Kaisel," she said carefully. "We need to talk."

 "About what?"

 "Sit down first."

 "Why?"

 Her expression softened.

 That scared me more than anything else.

 "Please."

 The room went quiet.

 I slowly let go of Elizabeth and followed them into the sitting room.

 Nobody sat comfortably.

 Roland stood near the fireplace with one hand resting against the mantle. Catherine sat near the edge of the sofa, her hands folded tightly together. Elizabeth sat beside me, close enough that her shoulder touched mine. Cedric and Amelia remained near the door.

 Nobody joked.

 Nobody argued.

 Nobody told me I was being too loud or too reckless or too stupid.

 Something was wrong.

 Very wrong.

 Roland took a slow breath.

 "Kaisel, three days ago, there was a massacre at House Dawnmere."

 The words barely finished leaving his mouth before I stood.

 "What?"

 Roland looked at me.

 "House Dawnmere was attacked during the gala."

 My ears rang.

 Dawnmere.

 Gala.

 Massacre.

 I didn't hear the rest.

 "Kaisel," Elizabeth said, grabbing my wrist.

 I pulled free without meaning to.

 "Finn?"

 Nobody answered fast enough.

 That was all I needed.

 I was already moving.

 "Kaisel!" Roland shouted.

 The front door slammed open before anyone could stop me.

 Then I ran.

 The streets blurred around me as I sprinted through the city. My body was still sore. My ribs still hurt. My legs burned almost immediately, but I didn't slow down.

 Finn.

 Finn.

 Finn.

 The name kept repeating in my head with every step.

 No.

 No, no, no.

 Not Finn.

 People shouted as I passed. A carriage swerved hard to avoid me. Someone yelled my name from a balcony. None of it mattered.

 Images forced themselves into my head.

 Finn laughing after I beat him in a spar and claiming he let me win.

 Finn stealing pastries from a banquet table and blaming me when we got caught.

 Finn standing beside me outside some boring noble event, whispering jokes under his breath until I nearly laughed in front of everyone.

 Finn, just weeks ago, telling me that I looked tired and pretending he wasn't worried.

 I ran faster.

 The Dawnmere estate came into view near the eastern side of the capital.

 Normally, it was bright.

 Lively.

 Guards in polished armor. Servants moving through the courtyard. Visitors coming and going through the front gates. Music sometimes drifting from inside if they were hosting something.

 Today it looked dead.

 Black cloth hung from the walls.

 The gates were open.

 Too many people stood around doing nothing.

 I ran up the front steps and slammed my fist against the door.

 "Finn!"

 Nothing.

 I hit it again.

 "Finn!"

 The door opened, and a servant stood there, face pale and eyes red like she had been crying for days.

 "Lord Kaisel—"

 I pushed past her.

 "Where is he?"

 "Lord Kaisel, please—"

 "Finn!"

 My voice echoed through the entrance hall.

 It was too quiet inside.

 The Dawnmere house had never been quiet. Not like this. There were always footsteps, voices, laughter, arguments, servants moving, someone calling for someone else from another room.

 Now there was just silence.

 Candles burned along the walls.

 Black ribbons hung from the banisters.

 A few servants stood in the hall, but none of them looked at me directly.

 "Finn!" I shouted again, moving deeper into the house.

 The servant hurried after me.

 "Lord Kaisel, please wait. You shouldn't run through here right now."

 I didn't stop.

 I turned down one hall, then another.

 "Finn!"

 Every room I passed looked wrong.

 Too clean.

 Too still.

 Too full of people who wouldn't answer me.

 My breath came faster.

 My thoughts got worse.

 Finn and I sparring in the courtyard.

 Finn grinning with a split lip and saying he almost had me.

 Finn sitting beside me on the floor of the Irvenest library because we had both been kicked out of some noble gathering.

 Finn saying, "You seriously need to teach me how to use that sword of yours."

 Me saying, "Maybe when you get taller than me."

 Him laughing.

 "Finn!"

 My voice cracked.

 I hated it.

 I hated that I sounded scared.

 I reached the end of the hallway and turned into the larger chamber near the back of the estate.

 Several people were gathered there.

 Servants.

 Guards.

 None of them spoke.

 Then a voice came from behind me.

 "Kaisel."

 I froze.

 Slowly, I turned.

 Tirian Dawnmere stood at the far end of the corridor.

 Finn's older brother.

 I had spoken with him a few times over the years. Not often, but enough to know him. He was usually composed, sharp, the kind of man who carried himself with pride.

 Now he looked broken.

 His clothes were neat, but he looked like he had slept in them. His eyes were bloodshot. His face was pale. His shoulders sagged like he was barely holding himself upright.

 I stared at him.

 He stared back.

 For a second, I couldn't ask.

 Then I forced the words out.

 "Where's Finn?"

 Tirian's face tightened.

 He didn't answer.

 My stomach dropped.

 "Tirian."

 His eyes lowered.

 "Kaisel…"

 I stepped toward him.

 "Where is he?"

 The room was silent.

 Every person in the hall seemed to be holding their breath.

 Tirian closed his eyes.

 "Please."

 His voice cracked.

 "Enough."

 That was it.

 That was all he had to say.

 My legs went weak.

 "No."

 Tirian didn't look at me.

 "No."

 The word came out again, smaller this time.

 I took one step back.

 Then another.

 My knees hit the floor.

 I didn't even feel it.

 Everything around me went quiet in a way I didn't understand. The servants. The candles. The room. Tirian. All of it faded until the only thing left was Finn's name repeating in my head.

 Finn.

 Finn.

 Finn.

 He was supposed to be here.

 He was supposed to laugh when I came bursting through the door.

 He was supposed to call me an idiot for panicking.

 He was supposed to be fine.

 My hands shook against the floor.

 I tried to breathe, but it came out wrong.

 Something wet hit my cheek.

 Then another.

 I realized I was crying only after I couldn't stop.

 Tirian walked over slowly and stopped in front of me, but he didn't say anything.

 Nobody did.

 There wasn't anything to say.

 Finn was dead.

 Outside, rain began tapping softly against the windows.

 For once, I didn't complain.
