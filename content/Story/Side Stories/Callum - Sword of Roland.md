# Sword of Roland

## Part I

Callum, Sword of Roland, sat atop the highest tower in the city of Thaylen and contemplated the void that encompassed his mind.

Of course, he didn't much care for the name those in [[The People's Liberation Front of Brittania|the Front]] had taken to calling him. He was no one's sword to swing.

The title had stuck regardless.

People needed names for things, boxes to sort them in.

Callum preferred not to fit in any.

The wind tugged at his scarf as he sat on the edge of the tower, one leg hanging over empty air. Far below, the city stretched out beneath him. Lamps burned in the streets and windows glowed with warm light. Somewhere down there, people laughed, drank, argued, lived their lives.

And somewhere down there was his target.

Lord Hector Vance.

A nobleman.

A cruel one.

Callum had read the reports. Heard the stories.

Crushing taxes that left families starving. Businesses extorted for coin, rivals quietly removed. The kind of man who treated people like resources instead of human beings.

He didn't know if every story was true, but enough of them were.

The man deserved to die.

That part was easy.

The harder part was the reason he was here in the first place.

The Front wanted the noble dead. In return, Roland had promised him information about the relics. He had discovered a lead — a clue about one of the relics Callum had been chasing for so long.

That was enough to overlook being given a chore. It wasn't like he was being ordered around, or they were treating him as though he was part of their cause, though he did align pretty closely to that.

It was just a simple exchange of services. They needed something, he needed something. Simple, pragmatic.

Callum wasn't a member of the Front. Never would be. And they knew that well. Respected it, even.

Organizations always wanted pieces of you. Loyalty, obedience, promises — just little things to bind you with.

He didn't like giving any of those things away.

But trading? That was different. There's no loss in that.

Trading made sense.

He killed one noble, Roland pointed him toward answers, everybody won.

His gaze drifted across the city.

The kingdom looked beautiful from up here.

The shining towers. The bustling streets. The banners hanging from stone walls.

Beautiful. Until you remembered what it looked like up close, underneath the pretty architecture and bustling streets.

No, this kingdom was rotting.

The feeling hit him every time he thought about it, a deep sickness in his gut.

An instinct. His body remembered something his mind didn't, he was sure of that now.

Whenever he looked at Brittania, he felt it.

This wasn't how a kingdom was supposed to be. Something had gone terribly, horribly wrong in the time he was asleep.

Something had twisted.

And somehow, impossibly, he felt like he was supposed to know why. The answer had to be in his memories.

It had to be.

A lifetime of memories, gone. Years, buried somewhere inside his skull.

There was something important there. Something connected to the relics. Something connected to the kingdom.

Maybe even something connected to him.

Whatever it was, he was sure that was the key to reviving this husk of a country.

Callum leaned back against the stone and stared up at the stars.

The frustrating part was being unable to explain any of this — not just to others, but to himself. Surely his memories must have value; Roland himself took Cal in on account of that.

But how can you explain the instinctual side of it without sounding crazy?

Still, he just knew.

The same way he knew how to fight despite never remembering being taught.

The same way his body retained the remarkable power he must have had before.

The scars that still marred his skin.

His body remembered. His instincts, his soul.

Only his mind had been left behind.

And that brought him to a question he found himself asking more often lately.

Who exactly would he be when he did remember?

The man before?

Or himself?

Would they be the same person?

At first, the answer had seemed obvious.

Of course they were.

But the man before had memories. Family, maybe. Friends. Goals.

Callum had none of that. He had woken up alone in a world he didn't recognize.

But the longer he thought about it, the less certain he became.

His instincts, his values, all seemed to come from that man.

The anger he felt when he looked at this kingdom came from that man.

The desire for freedom, the hatred of being controlled. The need to stand against things that felt oppressive.

How much of him had already been there?

What makes a man? Is it his memories, or is that just a piece of it?

Maybe the man from an age long forgotten, and the man sitting on this tower, were both Callum. In their own way.

Or maybe he was completely wrong.

Maybe recovering his memories would turn him into someone else entirely.

The thought should have frightened him. But strangely, it didn't.

Whatever waited in those memories, he needed to know, because every instinct in his body told him they were crucial.

And because if there really was a way to save this kingdom from whatever it had become, the answer was buried somewhere in the past.

Buried somewhere inside him.

A bell rang in the distance.

Callum glanced toward the noble's estate.

Several lights had gone dark.

The target was finally retiring for the night.

He stood and stretched. Questions could wait. Tonight there was work to do. And afterward, hopefully, answers.

With that thought, he pushed himself to his feet, and dropped off the edge of the tower.

## Part II

The manor was quieter than Callum expected.

Not silent. There were guards on patrol around the grounds and servants moving through the lower floors, but it was still well past midnight. Most of the estate had gone to sleep hours ago.

That made his job easier.

He crossed the rooftops overlooking the noble district with the same ease he crossed a road. The city stretched beneath him, lanterns illuminating narrow streets between rows of expensive homes. Lord Hector Vance's estate stood near the center of the district, larger than most of the surrounding manors and protected by a high stone wall that probably cost more than some villages earned in a year.

A fitting monument to the taxes he'd squeezed out of his subjects.

Callum stopped on a neighboring roof and studied the building. The study was on the third floor according to Roland's information. East wing, with large windows overlooking the city.

Easy enough.

A short leap carried him across the gap between the buildings. He landed on the manor's roof without a sound and quickly found the window he was looking for. It wasn't locked.

That almost felt insulting.

For a man who had spent decades making enemies, Vance seemed remarkably confident in his security.

Callum slipped inside.

The hall beyond was dimly lit by a few wall lanterns. Expensive paintings decorated the walls, alongside enough gold trim to make the entire corridor shine. He moved quietly toward the study, following the directions he'd memorized earlier that evening.

Two guards stood outside the study door, and both spotted him immediately.

One reached for his sword while the other opened his mouth to shout.

Neither got the chance.

Callum moved.

One moment he was standing at the end of the hallway. The next he was in front of them.

The first guard barely had time to widen his eyes before Callum drove a fist into his stomach.

The impact folded the man in half.

His feet left the floor entirely as the blow lifted him off the ground. Air exploded from his lungs and he crashed onto his back several feet away, his sword slipping from nerveless fingers as he curled up soundlessly on the floor.

The second guard reacted faster than most.

Not fast enough.

Steel hissed from its sheath as he swung toward Callum's neck.

Callum shifted his body just enough so that it passed harmlessly through the air in front of his face.

Without any particular sense of urgency, he reached out and caught the man's sword arm by the wrist.

The guard immediately tried to pull free. Nothing happened — his arm might as well have been trapped in a vice.

For the briefest moment confusion crossed the man's face as he realized he wasn't moving Callum at all.

Callum smirked slightly, and drove a knee into his stomach, knocking the air out of his lungs. As the guard doubled over, Callum brought his elbow down across the back of his neck.

The man collapsed instantly, and silence settled over the hallway once again.

Callum glanced at the first guard, who was still curled on the floor desperately trying to remember how breathing worked. Poor guy was just doing his job.

Then he stepped through the ruined doorway and entered the study.

Lord Hector Vance sat behind a large desk covered in papers and ledgers. The noble looked up in annoyance, likely expecting one of his guards, but the expression quickly vanished when he saw a stranger standing in the doorway with a sword at his side.

His eyes immediately flicked past Callum toward the hallway.

Toward the guards who weren't answering.

Understanding came quickly after that.

"The Front sent you."

Callum closed the door behind him.

"I'll take that as a yes."

The noble rose from his chair. Fear was there, but Callum could already see something else creeping in beneath it. Confidence. The confidence of a man who had spent his entire life solving problems by throwing money and influence at them.

"How much?" Vance asked.

Callum sighed. The question was becoming predictable at this point.

"I can pay more than they can. Double it. Triple it if I have to."

"You don't know what they offered."

"Then name a number."

Callum began to laugh.

"I'm sorry," Cal said, still grinning, regaining his composure.

"It's just that every noble I've gone through this song and dance with has said the same thing. No, I'm sorry, though — I'll have to say it to you too. It's only fair."

The relics. His memories. The answers he was chasing. None of those things could be bought from a noble's treasury.

"You don't have what I want."

The noble's face hardened.

"You have any idea who you're threatening?"

"Sorta."

"I advise you to find out."

"No thanks."

Callum continued walking forward.

Vance took a step backward.

"I sit on royal committees. I know governors. Generals. Merchants. Half the people who matter in this kingdom answer my letters."

"Congratulations."

Another step.

"If you kill me, every one of them will be looking for you."

"I'll count on it."

Another step, face calm, casual almost.

"You think the Liberation Front can protect you from that?"

"They don't protect me anyways."

Callum stopped a few feet from the desk.

The strange thing was that Vance genuinely believed what he was saying. He had spent so long surrounded by power that he couldn't imagine a world where it didn't matter.

"You can't do this," the noble snapped. "Do you understand who I am?"

Callum considered the question.

"A man who's about to die."

The noble's face went pale.

For the first time that night, he seemed to understand that none of his titles, wealth, or connections were going to save him.

A sword materialized in his hand.

A moment later, it was over.

The room fell silent, save for the steady drip of blood from his blade onto the beautiful hardwood floor.

Callum stood there for a few seconds, staring at the body.

He let himself feel some satisfaction at ridding the kingdom of another parasite.

Lord Hector Vance had made the world a worse place. Now he wasn't around to do it anymore. Net positive for the world, simple concept. Who could blame Cal for being happy about that.

He let the blade dissipate, gave the scene another look, and headed for the window.

Hopefully Roland's information would be worth the trouble.

Because if this clue led nowhere, Callum was going to have a very unpleasant conversation with him.
