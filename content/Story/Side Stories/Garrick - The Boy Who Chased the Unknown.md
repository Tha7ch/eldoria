## Characters & Locations

- Garrick's father — unnamed; trains Garrick in physical combat from childhood
- Garrick's mother — unnamed; tells stories of distant cities and magical wonders

**Locations**
- Unnamed elven village — Garrick's childhood home; edge of an ancient forest
- Unnamed mountain temple — ancient, overgrown, apparently abandoned; where the old monk trains; where the story ends

# Prologue: The Boy Who Chased the Unknown

Long before he discovered the forgotten temple hidden within the mountains, there lived a young elf named Garrick.

At sixteen years old, Garrick possessed a curiosity that seemed far too large for one person. While most children in his village were content to spend their days among familiar faces and familiar roads, Garrick constantly found himself wondering what existed beyond the horizon.

He wanted to know what lay beyond every mountain.

What secrets slept beneath ancient ruins.

What forgotten creatures roamed distant lands.

And what truths had been lost to time.

The world felt impossibly vast, and Garrick wanted to see every corner of it.

His parents often joked that one day he would simply wander off and accidentally discover a kingdom nobody knew existed.

The frightening part was that they were only half joking.

Garrick grew up in a humble elven household nestled near the edge of an ancient forest. The family was close, hardworking, and filled with stories.

Every evening, after supper, they gathered around the fireplace.

His father would tell tales of legendary heroes.

His mother would speak of distant cities built atop crystal cliffs.

Together they described dragons, enchanted forests, powerful sorcerers, and magical wonders scattered throughout the world.

Those stories fascinated Garrick more than anything.

Magic especially.

The thought that someone could summon fire from their hands or bend reality through sheer will captivated him.

One night, when he was still very young, he asked the question he had been holding back for months.

"Can either of you use magic?"

His parents exchanged glances before laughing.

His father shook his head.

"Not even a little."

Garrick turned to his mother.

"What about you?"

She smiled warmly.

"No magic here either."

His brow furrowed.

"But we're elves."

"We are."

"Aren't elves supposed to be good at magic?"

His mother chuckled.

"Some elves are."

His father leaned back in his chair.

"We simply weren't born with that gift."

The answer disappointed Garrick.

For a time, he even felt it was unfair.

Every story seemed filled with people capable of incredible magical feats while his family couldn't produce even the smallest spark.

Yet neither of his parents ever seemed bothered by it.

As Garrick grew older, he began to understand why.

His family had never focused on what they lacked.

Instead, they focused on what they could build.

Every morning before breakfast, the three of them trained together.

While other families slept, Garrick's family worked.

His father lifted heavy stones and chopped wood.

His mother practiced balance drills and agility exercises.

Garrick joined them in everything.

The training wasn't glamorous.

There were no magical powers.

No enchanted equipment.

No secret techniques.

Just discipline.

Effort.

And determination.

Whenever Garrick complained about a difficult exercise, his father would simply smile.

"Magic is a wonderful gift."

Then he would place a hand over his chest.

"But strength earned through effort belongs entirely to you."

Those words stayed with Garrick throughout his childhood.

If he couldn't rely on magic, he would become stronger.

If he couldn't cast spells, he would master his body.

The most important part of Garrick's day came every afternoon after lunch.

No matter how busy life became, his father always made time.

Together they would step into the yard behind their home and train.

At first, the lessons were simple.

Footwork.

Balance.

Basic strikes.

How to fall without getting hurt.

How to defend himself.

How to remain calm under pressure.

Then the sparring began.

And Garrick quickly learned just how difficult fighting could be.

His father threw him into the dirt countless times.

Every mistake was punished.

Every opening was exploited.

Every lesson was earned.

Whenever the training ended, Garrick would sit in the grass, exhausted and frustrated.

His father would offer him a hand and pull him back to his feet.

"Why did I lose?"

The answer always came immediately.

"You stopped watching my movements."

Or:

"You attacked before you were ready."

Or:

"You let your emotions control your decisions."

Years passed.

The lessons became harder.

The sparring became faster.

The boy became stronger.

Eventually Garrick began landing strikes of his own.

Then he started winning exchanges.

Then, on a warm summer afternoon, he finally managed to knock his father off balance and score a clean hit.

For a moment both stood frozen.

Then his father burst into laughter.

The proudest laughter Garrick had ever heard.

"About time."

Garrick never forgot that day.

His love for physical training was matched only by his love of learning.

Books filled his room.

Scrolls cluttered every shelf.

Maps hung from the walls.

Whenever merchants passed through town, Garrick eagerly searched their wares for anything interesting.

He studied everything he could find.

History.

Geography.

Ancient civilizations.

Monsters.

Kingdoms.

Languages.

But his favorite subjects were always martial arts.

He collected every scroll and manuscript related to combat he could get his hands on.

Some described elegant swordsmanship.

Others detailed ancient wrestling techniques.

Some taught disciplined military styles.

Others focused on speed and agility.

Garrick practiced all of them.

Every new technique fascinated him.

Yet no matter how much he learned, he couldn't shake a strange feeling.

Something was missing.

Every style seemed incomplete.

Useful.

Effective.

Even beautiful.

But not complete.

It felt as though he was searching for something he had never seen before.

Something that existed beyond the pages of books.

A style waiting to be discovered.

Then one day, shortly after his sixteenth birthday, Garrick set out to explore the mountains beyond his village.

What began as another ordinary adventure quickly became something far greater.

Deep within the wilderness, hidden beneath centuries of overgrowth and forgotten by nearly everyone, he discovered an ancient temple.

The structure appeared abandoned.

Cracked stone walls stood beneath tangled vines.

Weathered statues watched silently from their broken pedestals.

The gates hung open.

Curiosity immediately took hold.

Garrick stepped inside.

The air felt strangely calm.

Ancient.

Alive.

He wandered through silent hallways until a distant sound stopped him.

A sharp step.

A sudden turn.

The rush of air from a powerful strike.

Someone was here.

Moving quietly, Garrick followed the sounds until he reached a massive training hall supported by towering stone pillars.

Then he saw him.

An old monk stood alone at the center of the room.

And he was practicing a martial art unlike anything Garrick had ever witnessed.

The monk moved with impossible grace.

Every motion flowed seamlessly into the next.

His strikes were powerful yet controlled.

His kicks seemed to emerge from impossible angles.

His movements possessed a rhythm Garrick had never seen in any book or scroll.

The young elf's eyes widened.

He had spent years studying martial arts.

Years searching for something new.

Yet he had never seen anything remotely similar.

Quickly, he slipped behind one of the pillars, determined not to interrupt while continuing to watch.

The monk continued.

A spinning kick.

A sweeping strike.

A leap that seemed to ignore the limitations of age.

Minutes turned into nearly an hour.

Garrick couldn't look away.

His heart pounded with excitement.

This is it.

This is what I've been searching for.

Finally, the old monk completed his final movement and lowered his stance.

The hall fell silent.

Without turning around, the monk spoke.

"Did you enjoy the show?"

Garrick froze.

His stomach dropped instantly.

Slowly, the old monk turned his head toward the pillar.

A faint smile touched his face.

"You can come out now."

Embarrassed, Garrick stepped into view.

The monk studied him carefully.

"You've been watching for quite some time."

"I'm sorry," Garrick said quickly. "I didn't mean to intrude."

The monk chuckled.

"And yet you stayed."

Garrick nodded.

"I couldn't leave."

The old man seemed amused.

"What was it that interested you so much?"

Garrick didn't hesitate.

"That martial art."

The monk raised an eyebrow.

"What about it?"

"I've never seen anything like it."

His excitement became impossible to hide.

"I've studied countless fighting styles. I've read every scroll I could find. I've trained for years."

He clenched his fists.

"But what you were doing..."

He paused.

"...I've never seen anything like it."

The monk remained silent.

Garrick took a step forward.

"I think this is what I've been looking for."

The old man's eyes narrowed slightly.

Then came the question.

"Will you teach me?"

For several seconds, the monk simply stared.

Then he laughed.

Not cruelly.

Not mockingly.

Simply amused.

"You ask boldly."

"Maybe."

The monk crossed his arms.

"Do you know how many people have asked me that question?"

Garrick shook his head.

"Hundreds."

The old man turned away.

"They all wanted power."

His voice echoed through the hall.

"They all claimed they would work harder than anyone else."

He glanced back.

"And nearly all of them quit."

Garrick listened carefully.

"Words are easy, boy."

The monk's expression hardened.

"If I train you, your body will ache every day."

Garrick remained silent.

"You will wake before sunrise."

No response.

"You will push yourself beyond exhaustion."

Still nothing.

"There will be days when you question whether any of it is worth it."

For the first time, the monk saw something he hadn't expected.

Garrick wasn't frightened.

He was excited.

The old man studied him carefully.

"Tell me."

"Yes?"

"Why do you want this?"

The answer came instantly.

"Because I want to know what's beyond my limits."

The monk blinked.

Garrick smiled.

"I've spent my whole life exploring the world."

His eyes shone with determination.

"Every forest. Every hill. Every old ruin."

Then he placed a hand against his chest.

"And I think people are the same."

The monk listened.

"There are things hidden inside us."

Garrick looked around the ancient temple.

"Things we don't know are there."

His smile widened.

"I want to find them."

For a long moment, the old monk said nothing.

Then, finally, he nodded.

"Very well."

Hope immediately appeared on Garrick's face.

The monk raised a finger.

"Do not celebrate yet."

The smile vanished.

"You wish to learn this art."

"Yes."

"Then first, you must prove yourself."

Garrick straightened instantly.

"How?"

The old monk smiled.

This time, the smile looked almost dangerous.

"Tomorrow."

"Tomorrow?"

"Before sunrise."

Garrick swallowed.

"What happens tomorrow?"

The old monk's laughter echoed throughout the temple.

"A test."

"What kind of test?"

The monk turned and began walking away.

"The kind that will make you question whether you ever wanted to learn this martial art in the first place."

For the first time in years, the old monk felt intrigued.

And for the first time in his life, Garrick felt as though he had finally found the path he was meant to walk.

The real training had yet to begin.

But both of them knew that everything was about to change.
