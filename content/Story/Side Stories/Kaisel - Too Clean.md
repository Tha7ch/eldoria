# Part 2

 Five days later, I found Black Lantern Row.

 Well, I found it after getting lost twice, asking three different people for directions, and almost walking into a gambling den that I was pretty sure was also a front for knife fighting. So really, Black Lantern Row found me.

 The district sat lower than the rest of the city, almost like Britannia had pushed all its worst habits downhill and decided not to look at them anymore. The streets were narrow, wet, and packed with people who all looked like they had at least one weapon on them. Red lanterns hung from iron hooks above the road, their light dark and smoky as it spilled across stone walls, broken signs, and locked storefronts.

 This wasn't the kind of place where people hid what they were.

 Men with scars across their faces stood outside gambling houses. Women in expensive coats spoke quietly with masked figures in alleyways. Merchants sold items from covered tables and stopped talking whenever I got too close. Somewhere nearby, a man laughed so hard he started coughing, and nobody even turned to check if he was dying.

 Yeah. Definitely the right place.

 I walked down the middle of the street with one hand in my coat pocket and the other resting near the strap across my chest. My sword sat high across my back, the hilt near my shoulder where I could reach it easily. The dark blade moved with each step, familiar enough that I barely noticed the weight anymore.

 Other people noticed it though. They noticed me too. The whispers started before I made it ten steps. 

 "That's Irvenest."

 "The Chosen?"

 "What's he doing here?"

 "That's Kaisel."

 "Don't look at him too long."

 I tried not to smile. Failed. A little.

 Being known across Britannia came with a lot of annoying parts. People stared. Nobles whispered. Strangers thought they knew me because they'd heard stories. Every time I did something stupid, which was rarely, people acted like it meant something.

 But walking through a district full of criminals and watching them suddenly remember they had somewhere else to be?

 That part was pretty cool.

 A group of men standing outside a black-market stall went quiet as I passed. One of them had a knife in his hand, spinning it between his fingers like he thought that made him look dangerous. The second he saw me looking, he stopped spinning it and lowered his hand.

 I gave him a small nod. He looked away. Rude.

 The deeper I walked, the nicer the buildings became. Not cleaner. Not safer. Just richer. The broken gambling halls and cheap taverns gave way to private clubs with guarded doors, stone balconies, black curtains, and symbols carved above entrances that probably meant something to people who made money doing illegal things.

 Organized crime had a smell to it.

 Not literally.

 Well, sometimes literally.

 But mostly it was the way everyone moved. Nobody shouted unless they wanted attention. Nobody pulled a weapon unless they were ready to use it. People watched from windows, rooftops, doorways, and shaded corners, all pretending they weren't watching.

 I kept walking.

 Eventually, I reached the bathhouse.

 It stood at the far end of the row, larger than the buildings around it and made from dark stone that looked older than the street itself. Steam curled from narrow vents along the roof. Red lanterns hung on either side of the entrance, and a carved sign above the doorway had been painted black and gold.

 Two guards stood outside.

 Both were big, both armed, and both looked like they had been told smiling was bad for business.

 I walked up to them.

 The guard on the left stepped forward.

 "Closed."

 I looked past him at the bathhouse.

 "Looks open."

 "It's closed to you."

 "That's very specific."

 His face didn't change.

 I smiled. "I'm here for Veyr."

 The two guards exchanged a look.

 The one on the right folded his arms.

 "Is she expecting you?"

 "Nope."

 "Do you have an appointment?"

 "Nope."

 "Then leave."

 I stared at him for a second. Then I pointed at myself. "You guys know who I am, right?"

 That did it. Both of them went still.

 Not scared exactly, but definitely less confident than they were five seconds ago. The guard on the left looked me up and down again, like maybe he had somehow missed the sword, the coat, the face, and everything else people tended to remember.

 "Irvenest," he muttered.

 I snapped my fingers and pointed at him. "There you go."

 The other guard narrowed his eyes.

 "That doesn't mean you get to walk in here."

 "No, probably not." I shrugged. "But it does mean that if Veyr finds out Kaisel Irvenest came all the way here to talk to her and you two sent me away without even mentioning it, that might be bad for you."

 The guards didn't answer.

 I leaned slightly closer. "Maybe not. I don't know her that well. But from what I hear, people in your line of work really hate taking chances."

 The guard on the left stared at me for a long moment, then cursed under his breath and disappeared inside.

 The other stayed behind.

 We stood there in silence.

 He stared straight ahead.

 I looked around the street.

 "So," I said after a while, "you work here long?"

 He didn't answer.

 "Tough crowd."

 Still nothing.

 I glanced at his sword.

 "Nice blade."

 His eyes flicked toward me.

 "Don't."

 "Don't what?"

 "Talk."

 I raised both hands.

 "Fine. My bad."

 A few minutes later, the first guard returned. His expression was even more annoyed than before.

 "She's allowing it."

 I grinned.

 "See? Look at us solving problems."

 Neither guard moved.

 I cleared my throat. "Are you gonna open the door, or do I kick this one in too?"

 That got a reaction.

 The left guard opened the door.

 I stepped inside.

 The first floor of the bathhouse was quiet. Too quiet for a place sitting in the middle of Black Lantern Row. Polished wooden floors stretched beneath my boots, and warm lantern light reflected off dark stone walls. Men and women sat in private booths behind thin curtains, speaking in low voices. Servants moved between rooms carrying trays of food, wine, and folded towels.

 Nobody looked like they were relaxing.

 That was my first clue.

 A real bathhouse had noise. Water splashing. People talking. Someone laughing too loud. This place had none of that. Every conversation stopped when I passed.

 Every hand paused near a drink, a knife, a coin purse, or someone else's shoulder. I could feel eyes following me from behind screens and half-open doors. A few people recognized me right away. Others realized who I was after seeing the way everyone else reacted.

 Honestly, that was also pretty cool.

 A woman in black silk stepped in front of me and gave a short bow.

 "Lord Irvenest."

 "Just Kaisel."

 "Of course."

 She didn't look like she meant that. "Veyr is below. Follow me."

 She turned and led me through the bathhouse, past a row of private rooms and toward a wooden panel built into the back wall. She pressed her hand against a hidden latch, and the panel slid open to reveal a staircase leading down.

 Warm air rose from below.

 So did steam.

 I followed her down.

 The lower we went, the more the building changed. The polished wood gave way to older stone. The lanterns became fewer, placed in small iron cages along the walls. The air grew warmer and wetter until my coat started sticking slightly to my shoulders.

 At the bottom of the stairs, two more guards waited beside a set of double doors.

 One of them held out his hand.

 "Weapon."

 I looked at him.

 Then at my sword.

 Then back at him.

 "Really?"

 "Veyr's rule."

 I sighed and reached over my shoulder, unhooking the sword from the strap across my back. The moment the blade left its place, both guards stiffened like they expected me to swing it.

 I didn't.

 I flipped it carefully and handed it hilt-first to the guard.

 He took it with both hands.

 The weight nearly dragged his arms down.

 I smiled.

 "Careful."

 He adjusted his grip.

 I pointed at the blade.

 "Don't scratch it."

 The guard stared at me.

 "I'm serious."

  The woman in black silk gave me a look.

 I shrugged.

 "What? I like my sword."

 The doors opened.

 Steam rolled out immediately.

 The heat hit my face first, soft and thick, carrying the scent of hot water, scented oils, and stone warmed by fire. I stepped through the doorway and into a wide chamber filled with mist.

 The room was huge.

 Stone pillars rose from shallow pools. Lanterns hung from chains above, their golden light spreading through the steam in hazy circles. The water stretched across most of the room, divided by smooth stone walkways and low steps. Somewhere in the distance, water dripped steadily from a carved spout into one of the baths.

 For a few seconds, I didn't see anyone.

 Then the water moved.

 A figure sat near the center of the bath, mostly hidden by steam. As I walked closer, the mist shifted enough for me to make her out.

 Dark red hair, cut in a messy wolf-like style that framed her face and brushed just above her shoulders. Bright green eyes that watched me like she was deciding how much trouble I was worth. Freckles scattered across her cheeks and nose. A scar cut diagonally across her forehead and through one eyebrow, while another marked the bridge of her nose.

 She was attractive.

 Very attractive.

 Also probably dangerous.

 Usually those two went together.

 I stopped near the edge of the bath and gave a low whistle.

 "Okay."

 Veyr raised an eyebrow.

 "Is that how you greet every information broker you meet?"

 "Only the pretty ones."

 Her lips curved into a small smile, but her eyes stayed sharp.

 "You're seventeen."

 "I'm aware."

 "Good. Then I don't need to explain why that won't work."

 "Had to try."

 "No, you really didn't."

 I grinned and sat down on a stone bench near the edge of the water. My coat shifted around me as I leaned back and rested one arm along the bench.

 Veyr studied me openly. She didn't seem nervous. That was different.

 Most people in Black Lantern Row had looked at me like I was either a threat or a problem. Veyr looked at me like I was interesting, which somehow felt worse.

 "So," she said, resting both arms along the edge of the bath. "Kaisel Irvenest. Chosen of Wylder. Youngest Paladin in years. Son of House Irvenest. Famous enough to make half my guards panic by standing outside."

 I tilted my head.

  "Only half?"

 "The other half are stupid."

 "Fair."

 She smiled a little wider. "I'll admit, I didn't expect you to come here personally. People like you usually send messengers, servants, knights, or some poor idiot they can afford to lose."

 "People like me?"

 "Royal boys with famous names and holy titles."

 I laughed softly.

 "Yeah, that's not really my style."

 "No," Veyr said, eyes moving briefly to the door behind me. "I heard about the Crooked Boar."

 I leaned back slightly.

 "Already?"

 "Five days is a long time in my line of work."

 "Did they say I won?"

 "They said you destroyed the place."

 "Same thing."

 A quiet laugh escaped her. Then her expression shifted. Not dramatically. Just enough for me to notice. "You're not here to talk about taverns."

 The smile faded from my face. "No."

 The room seemed quieter after that. Steam drifted between us, softening the lantern light and making the rest of the bathhouse feel far away.

 I leaned forward, resting my elbows on my knees.

 "Aella Galefri."

 Veyr's face didn't change much at first.

 That told me more than if she had reacted.

 She knew the name.

 She had expected it.

 After a moment, she exhaled slowly and looked down at the surface of the water.

 "Of course."

 My fingers tightened slightly against each other.

 "You know about her."

 "Everyone knows about her."

 "That's not what I asked."

 Her eyes lifted back to mine.

 "No. I don't know what happened to her."

 The answer came too clean.

 Too ready.

 I didn't like it.

 "You didn't even think about that."

 "I didn't need to."

 "Why?"

 "Because I've asked myself that question more times than you have."

 That shut me up for a second.

 Veyr ran one hand slowly through the water, creating small ripples that spread out and vanished into the steam.

 "When Aella Galefri disappeared, half of Britannia talked about it. A daughter of one of the kingdom's most important families vanishes without a trace. No ransom. No body. No witnesses anyone trusted. No proof of escape. No public enemy taking credit. Nothing."

 She looked back at me.

 "Do you know how rare that is?"

 I didn't answer.

 She continued anyway.

 "It's almost impossible. People leave trails. Money moves. Servants talk. Guards drink too much. Carriages get seen. Letters get intercepted. Someone always knows something, and if someone knows something, someone like me can usually buy it."

 Her voice stayed calm, but there was a slight edge to it now.

 "With Aella, there was nothing."

 I watched her carefully.

 "You looked into it."

 "Of course I did." She sounded almost offended.

 "One of the biggest mysteries in Britannia happens practically in front of every noble family, and you think I was going to ignore it? I had people searching through travel records, guard routes, servant gossip, smuggling paths, old tunnels, hidden roads, criminal ledgers, everything."

 She leaned forward a little. "I paid people who hated me. I threatened people who feared me. I listened to drunk nobles lie badly at parties, and I listened to criminals lie even worse in alleys. For months, every lead turned into dust."

 My chest felt tighter than it had a moment ago.

 That wasn't what I wanted to hear.

 I didn't care if Kane was useless because he was Kane.

 I didn't want Veyr to be useless too.

 "And you're telling me you found nothing."

 Veyr watched me.

 "Nothing useful."

 "That's the same thing."

 "No, it isn't."

 She tapped one finger lightly against the stone edge of the bath. "Finding nothing can tell you plenty if you know what nothing is supposed to look like."

 I frowned.

 "Explain."

 Veyr seemed to like that.

 She shifted slightly in the water and leaned one shoulder against the bath's edge.

 "If traffickers had taken her, there would have been movement. A royal girl is valuable, especially one with Galefri blood. Someone would've tried to sell her, ransom her, trade her, hide her, or use her."

 Her green eyes sharpened. "I would've heard a whisper."

 I stayed quiet.

 "If a rival family had taken her, there would've been pressure. Political movements. Quiet threats. Shifts in alliances. Someone gaining something from her disappearance."

 She shook her head. "There was none of that either."

 I leaned forward more.

 "Then what are you saying?"

 "I'm saying the whole thing was too clean."

 The words sat between us.

 Too clean.

 I didn't like the sound of that.

 Veyr must have seen it on my face because she didn't soften her tone at all.

 "Whatever happened to Aella Galefri wasn't some random kidnapping. It wasn't a gang snatching a noble girl off the street. It wasn't one of my people, and it wasn't any organization I know."

 "Then who?"

 She looked at me for a long moment.

 Then said it.

 "Someone inside House Galefri."

 I didn't move.

 For a second, all I heard was water dripping somewhere behind me.

 "No."

 Veyr didn't look surprised by the answer.

 Most people probably said no when they heard something they didn't want to. I stood slowly from the bench. "That doesn't make sense."

 "It makes more sense than every other answer."

 "No, it doesn't."

 "Yes, it does." Her voice remained calm. That made it worse.

 "If someone outside the family knew what happened, I would have found something. A name. A payment. A route. A guard who looked away. A witness too scared to talk unless enough gold was involved. Anything."

 She held my stare.

 "But if I found nothing, then the people who knew were already inside the walls."

 I looked away.

 Whiro's name came to mind immediately.

 I hated that.

 I hated how fast it happened.

 "Whiro would've told me."

  Veyr tilted her head slightly.

  "He would've told me," I repeated.

  "Maybe."

 I looked back at her. "Not maybe."

 Veyr's expression softened slightly, though not enough to make her seem gentle.

 "Kaisel, I'm not saying he did anything to her."

 "Then what are you saying?"

 "I'm saying people keep secrets for different reasons." She kept going. "Some keep secrets because they're guilty. Some keep secrets because they're afraid. Some keep secrets because they think knowing the truth will hurt someone worse than not knowing."

 My jaw tightened.

 "He wouldn't do that."

 "You sure?"

 "Yes."

 Veyr studied me quietly. She didn't argue immediately, which was somehow more irritating than if she had.

 After a moment, she said, "You came all the way to Black Lantern Row because you didn't have answers. You fought through Kane Orso's tavern because you thought he might know something. Now you're sitting in front of me, and I'm telling you where the trail points."

 She paused.

 "You don't have to like it."

 I stared at her. I exhaled sharply and ran a hand through my hair.

 The steam made it damp around my forehead. I pushed a few white strands with brown streaks out of my eyes and tried to think clearly.

 Whiro wouldn't hide something from me. He wouldn't. 

 Probably.

 No.

 He wouldn't.

 Veyr watched all of that play across my face. She was too good at reading people. "Why does she matter this much to you?"

 I looked at her.

 The question wasn't mocking.

 That surprised me.

 Veyr leaned back again, green eyes steady on mine.

 "It's been five years. People go missing in Britannia every day. Nobles, servants, merchants, children, criminals, people with names, people without them. Most people give up because they have to. You clearly haven't."

 I didn't answer right away.

 For once, I didn't have something quick to say.

 The steam drifted between us. The bathhouse felt quieter now.

 Finally, I said, "She's my best friend."

 Veyr didn't interrupt.

 "She was there from the beginning. Before all the titles, before I was Chosen, before I became a Paladin, before I became special to this world. She saw me as me."

 I frowned slightly, not because I was angry at Veyr, but because saying it out loud felt weird.

 "I don't care if it's been five years. I don't care if everyone else thinks she's gone. If there's even a chance she's alive, I'm going to find her."

 Veyr's expression changed.

 Only a little.

 The sharpness stayed, but something quieter appeared behind it.

 "That's a dangerous way to live."

 I shrugged.

 "Probably."

 "You could spend years chasing nothing."

 "Maybe."

 "You could find out something you don't want to know."

 "Probably."

 "And you'll still keep going?"

 "Yeah." I said it immediately. There wasn't really another answer.

 Veyr stared at me for a while. Then she smiled. It wasn't teasing this time. It was smaller.

 Almost approving. "You're either very loyal or very stupid."

 "Both, sometimes."

 The tension in the room loosened a little, but not completely.

 I looked back toward the doorway.

 I had what I needed.

 Not answers.

 Not even a real lead.

 But I had a direction I didn't have before.

 House Galefri.

 Whiro.

 Whatever was hidden inside that family.

 I didn't like it.

 But I wasn't going to ignore it.

 I started toward the door.

 "Kaisel."

 I stopped and looked back.

 Veyr had shifted in the bath, her expression turning more serious again. She no longer looked like the amused information broker who had been teasing me earlier. For a moment, she looked tired.

 Actually tired.

 "I need something from you."

 I raised an eyebrow. "That's a dangerous sentence coming from you."

 "It isn't illegal."

 "That was going to be my next question."

 She reached toward the edge of the bath and picked up a folded slip of paper. Water ran down her arm as she held it between two fingers.

 "My younger sister lives not far from here. Different district. Quieter. Safer, supposedly."

 I walked back toward the edge of the bath and took the paper from her.

 There was an address written on it.

 "And?"

 "I haven't heard from her in weeks."

 The way she said it made me look up.

 Veyr's face was calm, but her fingers tapped lightly against the stone. Once. Twice. Three times.

 Nervous.

 She didn't seem like the type who got nervous often.

 "I sent people," she continued. "Men I trust. Women I trust more. People who owe me enough not to lie. They went to her house, asked around, checked the area, and came back with nothing useful."

 I looked down at the address again.

 "What's her name?"

 "Mira." The name came softer than the rest.

 That told me plenty. "Why me?" I asked.

 Veyr met my eyes.

 "Because if I send more of my people, whoever is watching her house will see them coming. If I send mercenaries, they'll make noise. If I go myself, half of Black Lantern Row will know before I reach the front door."

 "And me?"

 "You're already loud."

 I blinked.

 She smiled faintly.

 "But in a different way."

 "I don't know if that's an insult."

 "Kaisel." Her tone shifted just enough to make me stop. "I need someone who will actually look for her, not just report what they think I want to hear."

 For a moment, I said nothing.

 The paper felt heavier in my hand than it should've. I had come here for Aella.

 Not Veyr.

 Not her sister.

 Not another missing person.

 But that was the problem.

 Another missing person.

 I folded the note and slipped it into my coat.

 "Alright."

 Veyr stared at me. "Just like that?"

 "Yeah."

 "You don't want payment?"

 "Not really."

 "You don't want to ask what kind of trouble you might be walking into?"

 "I probably should."

 "But you won't?"

 "I'll figure it out when I get there."

 Veyr shook her head slowly.

 I pointed at her.

 "But just so we're clear, if this turns into some weird crime thing, I'm coming back and arresting you."

 She threw up her hands in a playful gesture, "Sure thing, Chosen."

 "I'm serious. No smuggling, no intimidation, no breaking into innocent people's houses, no weird black-market nonsense."

 "Noted."

 "And if your sister is in danger, I'm helping her first."

 Veyr's expression softened again."That's why I'm asking you."

 I nodded once. "Then I'll find her."

 For the first time since I'd arrived, Veyr didn't look amused, dangerous, or impossible to read. She just looked relieved. "Thank you."

 I turned toward the exit again.

 As I reached the door, one of the guards opened it from the other side. Warm steam poured out into the hall around me.

 Before stepping through, I glanced back at Veyr. "By the way."

 She raised an eyebrow.

 "If your guards scratched my sword, I'm blaming you."

 The small smile returned.

 "Goodbye, Kaisel."

 "Yeah, yeah." I stepped into the hallway and let the door close behind me.

 A few minutes later, my sword was back across my shoulder, thankfully unscratched, and I was walking out of the bathhouse into Black Lantern Row.

 The red lanterns still burned above the street. The criminals still watched from the shadows. The whispers started again the moment they saw me. But I barely paid attention this time.

 Aella.

 Whiro.

 House Galefri.

 Mira.

  One mystery had turned into two. I sighed and started down the street. "Great," I muttered to myself. "Because one missing girl definitely wasn't enough."
