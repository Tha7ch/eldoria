# ***Age 17:***

 ***Part 1***

 The district was darker than I expected.

 Most places in Britannia had at least a few lanterns burning through the night, even in the rougher areas. Here, half of them were broken, and the rest gave off weak yellow light that barely reached the ground. The streets were narrow, the buildings leaned too close together, and the rain from earlier still sat in the cracks between the stones.

 I walked with one hand in my coat pocket and the other resting near the strap across my chest. My sword was on my back, heavy and familiar, the dark blade wrapped behind my shoulder.

 Kane Orso.

 That was the name I had been given.

 I didn't know if it would actually lead anywhere, but it was better than standing around and doing nothing. If Kane knew something, great. If he didn't, then maybe he knew somebody who did. Either way, I was already here, and walking back empty-handed sounded annoying.

 A few people passed me on the street. Most kept their heads down. Some looked at me once, noticed the sword, and quickly decided whatever they were doing was more important than staring. 

 I stopped near a corner where two alleys split off in opposite directions. The tavern was supposed to be around here somewhere, but every building looked like it had been built by someone who hated signs.

 A girl about my age stepped out from under a cracked stone archway, carrying a small basket under one arm. She had dark hair tied back messily and a cloak pulled tight around her shoulders. The moment she saw me, she slowed down.

 I lifted a hand.

 "Hey. Quick question."

 She froze for half a second, then looked around like she was checking if I was talking to someone else.

 "Me?"

 "Yeah, you. Do you know where the Crooked Boar is?"

 Her eyes widened.

 "The Crooked Boar?"

 "That's what I said."

 She looked me up and down. Her gaze stopped on my sword, then went to my face. A second later, her expression changed.

 "Wait… aren't you Kaisel Irvenest?"

 A small smile formed on my face. "Try to keep my identity a secret." Winking at her as I put a finger to my lips. 

 She blinked, then quickly shook her head.

 I pointed down the street before she could get more excited. "Crooked Boar?"

 Her expression tightened a little. She glanced past me, toward the darker part of the road.  "You shouldn't go there."

 My eyebrow raised slightly at that, "And why's that?"

 Her voice dropped lower. "Bad people go there. And not just thieves, but killers, bandits, gangs…"

 That sounded about right.

  I gave her a small nod.

  "Then I'm probably in the right place."

 She hesitated, then pointed to the alley on my left.

 "Down there. Take the stairs at the end, turn right, and you'll see a building with a boar skull over the door."

 "Thanks."

 I started walking, but she called after me.

 "Be careful."

 I looked back at her and gave her a grin.

 The alley was tighter than the street. Water dripped from the rooftops above, splashing against my shoulders and the top of my head. The place smelled like wet stone, smoke, and something rotten that I didn't want to identify.

 At the end of the alley, I found the stairs. They led down into a lower street where the lanterns burned red instead of yellow. The Crooked Boar sat at the far end, pressed between two taller buildings. A large animal skull hung above the door, cracked down the middle and nailed into the wood.

 Classy.

 The tavern was loud from the outside. Men shouting, mugs hitting tables, someone laughing too hard, and music that sounded like three different songs fighting each other.

 I rolled my shoulders once and stepped inside.

 The noise didn't stop right away. It faded in pieces.

 First the table near the door went quiet. Then the card game beside them. Then the men at the counter. By the time I took three steps in, most of the room was staring at me while pretending they weren't.

 The tavern was packed. Rough-looking men and women filled the tables. Some had scars across their faces. Some had weapons sitting openly beside their drinks. A few wore hoods even though they were indoors, which was always a great way to look suspicious.

 The air was warm, thick, and smelled like cheap ale.

 I walked straight to the counter.

 The bartender was a big man with a shaved head and arms thick enough to look annoying in a fight. He was cleaning a mug with a dirty rag, which felt pointless considering the mug looked cleaner than the rag.

 He didn't look at me when I sat down.

 "What do you want?"

 "Information."

 "Don't sell that."

 "Shame. You look like a business man."

 His eyes flicked up to me.

 I leaned an elbow on the counter and smiled.

 "I'm looking for Kane Orso."

 The bartender's face didn't change, but the room did.

 A chair creaked behind me. Someone stopped whispering. One guy at the far table looked toward the stairs before quickly pretending he hadn't.

 The bartender set the mug down. "Never heard of him."

 "Really?" 

 "Really."

 I nodded slowly and looked around the room. "That's weird." I turned back to the bartender. "Because the air in here just got a bit heavy at the mention of his name."

 His jaw tightened. "Kid, you should leave."

 "Probably." I tapped my fingers on the counter. "But I'm not going to."

 A man behind me stood up. I heard the scrape of his chair against the floor before I saw him. He was tall, broad, and smelled like he'd lost a fight with a brewery.

 "You deaf?" he said. "Man told you to leave."

 I turned my head slightly. "Sorry but I'm talking right now."

 He grabbed my shoulder.

 I looked down at his hand, then back at him. "What are you gonna do, big guy?"

 He swung first.

 I ducked under his fist, grabbed his wrist, and drove my elbow into his stomach. The air left him in one ugly cough. Before he could fall, I caught the neck of his shirt and slammed him face-first into the counter.

 The whole tavern went silent.

 The bartender reached under the bar.

 I grabbed his wrist before he could pull whatever he was reaching for. "Don't."

 He tried anyway.

 I sighed, yanked him forward, and slammed his forehead into the counter. Not hard enough to crack anything. Hard enough to make him stop being annoying.

 He dropped behind the bar with a groan.

 For about two seconds, nobody moved.

 Then the room exploded.

 Three men rushed me at once.

 The first came in with a bottle. I stepped to the side, caught his arm, and twisted until the bottle dropped. I kicked it away, grabbed him by the collar, and threw him over the nearest table. Drinks flew everywhere as he crashed into two of his friends.

 The second tried to punch me in the jaw. I leaned back just enough for his fist to miss, then stepped in and drove my knee into his stomach. He folded forward, and I brought my elbow down across his back.

 The third actually had a knife.

 "Okay," I said, grabbing his wrist before the blade reached me. "Didn't know weapons were on the table."

 I twisted his arm, knocked the knife loose, and headbutted him. He dropped to the floor and stayed there.

 A chair came flying at me from the left.

 I caught it with both hands.

 The man who threw it stared at me.

 I stared back and smiled at him.

 Then I threw it back.

 He went down with a crash, the chair breaking across his chest. He wasn't dead. Probably. He was breathing, so.

 More of them stood up.

 I looked around the room and raised both hands. "Just so we're clear, this is self defense."

 Someone shouted and charged.

 I stepped forward to meet him.

 The next few minutes were mostly noise.

 A fist glanced off my cheek. I grabbed the man who threw it and shoved him backward into another thug. Someone tried to tackle me from the side, so I hooked an arm around his neck, spun, and tossed him into a table hard enough to split it in half.

 A woman with a shaved head came at me with a short club. I ducked the first swing, caught the second on my forearm, and winced. "Ow."

 She swung again.

 I kicked her knee out from under her, caught her before she hit the ground too hard, then gently dropped her on her face. "That one actually hurt."

 She groaned something into the floor.  

 "Yeah, yeah. You did great."

 A man climbed onto a table and jumped at me.

 I stepped aside.

 He hit the floor behind me with a heavy thud.

 I turned and looked down at him. "Why would you do that?"

 He didn't answer.

 Fair enough.

 Then the weapons came out.

 Short swords, knives, clubs, one rusty axe, and something that looked like a farming tool but probably wasn't being used for farming anymore.

 I looked at all of them.

 Then I looked at the sword on my back.

 "Alright."

 I reached up and grabbed the hilt.

 The room slowed down a little as I drew the blade free. The dark metal caught the tavern light, and the red gems along the guard glowed faintly. A few people stepped back the moment they saw it.

 Good.

 At least some of them had survival instincts.

 I rested the flat of the blade across my shoulder.

 "Last chance to sit down."

 Nobody sat down.

 I shrugged.

 "Your funeral." Then I paused. "Not actually. Bad wording."

 The first armed man came in fast. I turned my blade sideways and caught his sword against mine, then shoved his weapon down and slammed the flat of my blade into his ribs. He dropped with a sharp gasp.

 Another swung at my head. I ducked and cut through the leather strap holding his weapon sheath. His belt sagged, his pants dropped slightly, and he stumbled.

 I kicked him in the chest and sent him rolling across the floor.

 A knife came at my back. I heard the step, turned, and pinned the attacker's sleeve to a wooden pillar with the very tip of my sword. He froze, eyes wide.

 I leaned close.

 "Stay."

 He nodded quickly.

 "Good boy."

 I moved on.

 I didn't swing at their necks. I didn't aim for chests. I used the flat of the blade, the guard, the pommel, my elbows, my knees, whatever ended the fight without ending the person.

 That made things harder.

 It was still fun.

 A man with the rusty axe swung low at my leg. I hopped over it, landed on the handle, and kicked him across the face. He dropped the axe and fell backward into a stack of barrels.

 Someone behind the bar fired a crossbow.

 The bolt skimmed past my shoulder and buried itself in the wall.

 I slowly turned.

 The man holding the crossbow swallowed.

 "That was rude."

 He tried to reload.

 I threw a mug at him. It hit him square in the forehead, and he disappeared behind the counter. A little laugh came out from that. 

 The tavern was a complete mess now. Tables were broken. Chairs were broken. Half the people were on the floor. The other half were either crawling away or pretending they hadn't been involved.

 I stepped over a man groaning into a puddle of ale and walked toward the stairs.

 A hand grabbed my ankle.

 I looked down.

 It was the first guy from the counter. Somehow, he was still awake.

 "Don't go upstairs," he muttered.

 I smiled. "See, now I definitely have to."

 I gently kicked his hand off and continued up.

 The stairs creaked under my boots. The music downstairs had stopped completely, replaced by groans and the occasional sound of someone trying to crawl without being noticed.

 At the top of the stairs, two guards stood outside a door.

 They both stared at me.

 I stared back.

 One of them looked past me toward the destroyed tavern below.

 Then back at me.

 "You're not supposed to be up here," he said.

 I nodded.

 "Yeah, people keep saying stuff like that tonight."

 The guard on the left drew a short sword.

 The guard on the right lifted a club.

 I adjusted my grip on my sword.

 "Are we doing this?"

 They rushed me.

 I stepped onto the wall.

 For one second, both of them looked confused. That was enough.

 I kicked off the wall, spun over the first guard's swing, and landed behind him. The flat of my blade cracked against the back of his knee, and he dropped. Before the second could turn, I grabbed his wrist, twisted the club out of his hand, and slammed the pommel of my sword into his stomach.

 He bent forward.

 I patted his shoulder.

 "Good talk."

 Then I slammed his head into the wall.

![[Kaisel slamming someones head into a table at a bar.webp|450]]

 He slid down beside the other one.

 I checked both of them quickly. Breathing. Awake enough to regret things. Fine.

 I turned to the door.

 Voices came from inside. A man's voice, low and irritated. A woman's voice, softer. Then a laugh.

 I kicked the door open.

 It slammed against the wall hard enough to crack the wood.

 The room inside was larger than I expected. A wide bed sat against the far wall, covered in red sheets and too many pillows. Candles burned on a table near the window. Empty bottles were scattered across the floor.

 Kane Orso sat up in bed, shirt half-open, eyes wide.

 Two women screamed.

 I raised one hand.

 "You're fine. Leave."

 They didn't need to be told twice.

 They grabbed what clothes they could and ran past me into the hallway. I stepped aside to let them go, keeping my eyes on Kane the whole time.

 Once they were gone, I made a whistle sound. "Didn't know Kane Orso was a ladies man. Two fine ladies at that." A smirk formed on my face while we stared each other down.

 The atmosphere in the room became so heavy, it was hard not to notice it. Then Kane reached under the pillow.

 In that instance, I threw my sword.

 The blade flew across the room and pierced through the blanket beside his leg, sinking deep into the wooden frame of the bed.

 Kane froze.

 The edge of the blade had cut his thigh, but not deep. Enough to hurt. Enough to stop him from moving. Not enough to make him bleed out.

 He stared at the sword, then at me.

 I walked fully into the room.

 "Kane Orso."

 He swallowed.

 "Who are you?"

 I looked at him.

 He looked at the sword.

 Then back at me.

 His face changed. "Oh."

 "Yeah."

 I pulled the chair closer to the bed and sat down, resting one arm across the backrest. The chair creaked under my weight as I studied Kane. 

 He looked nervous, but not terrified. That annoyed me a little. Most people got a lot more nervous when a sword was sticking through their mattress.

 "So let's start simple."

 Kane didn't answer.

 I leaned forward slightly.

 "What do you know about Aella Galefri?"

 For the first time since I'd entered the room, his expression genuinely changed. His eyebrows pulled together slightly as he stared at me.

 "Aella Galefri?"

 "Yeah."

 "The Galefri girl?"

 My heart skipped a beat.

 I immediately sat up straighter.

 "You know her?"

 Kane shook his head.

 "No. I know *of* her."

 The excitement I'd felt immediately started slipping away.

 "What does that mean?"

 "It means exactly what I said." Kane shifted against the headboard before continuing. "Everybody in Britannia heard about that story. Royal family. Young daughter. Disappears without a trace. Half the kingdom spent months talking about it."

 I stared at him.

 "That's it?"

 "What do you mean, that's it?"

 "I mean that's all you know?"

 Kane laughed.

 Not because he thought it was funny. Because he thought I was being unreasonable. "You're looking at me like I'm supposed to have some secret answer."

 "You traffic people."

 His laughter stopped.

 "You work with criminals."

 His jaw tightened.

 "You know gangs, smugglers, brokers, merchants, bounty hunters, and every other piece of trash that makes money moving people around Britannia." I pointed at him. "So yeah. I expected you to know something."

 For a moment, Kane didn't answer. He simply looked at me. Then he let out a long sigh and rubbed a hand over his face. "You know what your problem is?"

 I raised an eyebrow. "I have several. Be specific."

 "You already decided I know something before you walked into this room."

 I didn't answer. Because he wasn't completely wrong.

 Kane noticed.

 A small smirk formed on his face.

 "See? That's exactly what this is."

 His gaze shifted toward the floor for a moment before returning to me.

 "You came here wanting me to be the answer. Doesn't matter what I say. Doesn't matter what I tell you. You want me to be connected to her because if I am, then you finally have somewhere to go."

 The room grew quiet.

 I hated how accurate that sounded.

 "So you're saying you know nothing."

 "I'm saying I've never met her."

 "Never heard anything?"

 "Nothing useful."

 "Rumors?"

 "Everybody heard rumors."

 "What kind of rumors?"

 Kane shrugged.

 "Different ones. Some people said she was kidnapped. Some people said she ran away. Some people said she was dead."

 His eyes met mine.

 "I don't believe any of those."

 I folded my arms.

 "Why not?"

 "Because if anybody actually knew what happened to her, we wouldn't still be talking about her five years later."

 That was probably the smartest thing he'd said all night.

 I hated that too.

 For a few seconds, neither of us spoke.

 The candles flickered quietly. Rain tapped against the window. Somewhere downstairs a man groaned loud enough that I recognized one of my earlier punches.

 Then Kane sighed.

 "If you're looking for somebody who deals in information, you're barking up the wrong tree."

 I looked at him.

 "What does that mean?"

 "It means I move people."

 He gestured toward himself.

 "I move cargo. I move products. I move things from one place to another."

 His expression darkened.

 "But information?"

 He shook his head.

 "That's somebody else's business."

 I immediately noticed the wording.

 "Somebody."

 Kane froze.

 Then muttered a curse under his breath.

 A grin spread across my face. "There it is."

 "I hate talking to Paladins."

 "I'm not even talking like a Paladin right now."

 "You're worse." Kane rubbed his forehead.

 For a moment it looked like he was debating whether telling me was worth it.

 Eventually he lost the argument with himself.

 "Her name's Veyr."

 I sat forward immediately.

 "Who is she?"

 "The person you should've been looking for instead of me."

 Kane pointed toward the window.

 "Information broker. Knows everything. Or at least enough to make people think she does."

 "And she might know something about Aella?"

 "If anyone does, it'll be her."

 "Where do I find her?"

 Kane stared at me.

 I stared back.

 Neither of us moved.

 Finally, he sighed.

 "Black Lantern Row. Old bathhouse. Lower floor."

 I smiled.

 Finally.

 Now we were getting somewhere.

 I pulled my sword free from the bedframe and rested it across my shoulder. The wood cracked loudly as the blade slid free, leaving a deep hole through the mattress and frame.

 Kane watched me carefully as I turned toward the door.

 For a moment, neither of us said anything.

 The conversation was over.

 I had what I came for.

 Veyr.

 Black Lantern Row.

 Old bathhouse.

 That was enough.

 I took a few steps toward the doorway before stopping.

 "Oh."

 Kane frowned. "What?"

 I glanced back over my shoulder. "One more thing."

 His eyes narrowed slightly.

 I pointed at him.

 "You should turn yourself in."

 Silence.

 Kane stared at me. I stared back. A few seconds passed. Then Kane let out a short laugh. Not because he thought it was funny. Because he thought I was joking. "Wait." He shook his head. "You're serious?"

 I nodded. "Yep."

 "Why would I willingly walk into a prison cell?"

 I shrugged.

 "Because you're a bad person."

 The answer came so naturally that Kane actually looked caught off guard.

 "You traffic people. You work with gangs. You help criminals make money. If I wanted to, I could've walked in here and killed you."

 The room grew quiet.

 I shifted the sword slightly on my shoulder.

 "But I didn't."

 Kane didn't say anything.

 "So the best thing you can probably do now is turn yourself in."

 For a second, Kane just stared.

 Then he laughed again.

 Not because it was funny.

 Because he genuinely couldn't believe what he was hearing.

 "And you actually expect me to do that?"

 I nodded.

 "Yeah."

 "Why?"

 I shrugged.

 The answer seemed obvious. "Because it's the right thing to do."

 Silence filled the room.

 Kane opened his mouth.

 Then closed it again.

 For once, he didn't seem to have anything to say.

 I smiled. "Anyway, think about it." Then I turned around and walked out into the hallway.

 The last thing I saw before leaving was Kane still sitting on the bed, staring at the doorway with the same confused expression he had when I'd first told him.
